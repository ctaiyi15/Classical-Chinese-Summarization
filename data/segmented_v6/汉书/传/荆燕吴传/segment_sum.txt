translation:
The year after Liu Ze became King Langxie, Empress Dowager Lu died of illness. Liu Ze then said: The emperor is young, Zhu Lu is in power, and the Liu clan is lonely and weak.
So he led his troops and conspired with the King of Qi to march westward, intending to kill Zhu Lu.
When he arrived in Liangdi, he heard that General Guan Ying of the Han Dynasty was stationed in Rongyang, so he withdrew his troops and stationed them in the western frontier of his country, and then rushed to Chang'an alone.
At this time, Liu Heng, the king of Dai, also arrived in Chang'an, the capital of Dai. The generals and ministers and Liu Ze jointly supported the king of Dai as the emperor of the Han Dynasty, that is, Emperor Xiaowen.
In the first year of Emperor Xiaowen's reign, Liu Ze was renamed King of Yan. The Langye area was still given to Qi.
Liu Ze died after two years as King of Yan, and Mi paid tribute to him.
His son Kang Wang Liu Jia succeeded to the throne and died in the ninth year.
Dingguo, the son of King Kang, succeeded to the throne.
Dingguo committed adultery with his father's concubine. She gave birth to a boy, took his sister-in-law as a concubine, and committed adultery with her three daughters.
Dingguo planned to kill the Ying people, the magistrate of Feiru County. The Ying people and others reported to the court, and Dingguo sent envoys to kill the Ying people and silence them for violating other laws.
During the Yuanshuo period, the brothers of the Ying people once again wrote to the court to report Dingguo's crimes in detail.
Emperor Wu asked the ministers to discuss it, and they all said: Dingguo's behavior is like an animal, messing up human relations and violating the will of God, and he should be punished with death.
The emperor approved this proposal.
Dingguo then committed suicide. Forty-two years after the establishment of the feudal state, it was abolished.
During the reign of Emperor Ai, in order to continue the vassal family that had cut off the feudal state, Liu Guisheng, Liu Ze's great-great-grandson's grandson from Wuzhong County, who had only a first-class title, was granted the title of Marquis of Yingling. During the Gengshi period, Liu Guisheng was killed by soldiers.
King Wu Liu Bi was the son of Liu Zhong, brother of Emperor Gao.
Emperor Gao made Liu Zhong the acting king.
When the Xiongnu attacked Dai, Liu Zhong could not hold on, so he secretly abandoned the country and fled, returning to the capital Chang'an from Luoyang. The emperor could not bear to bring him to justice, so he deposed him from the throne and demoted him to the rank of Marquis of Heyang.
His son Liu Bi was granted the title of Marquis of Pei.
King Yingbu of Huainan rebelled, and Emperor Gaozu personally led troops to attack him.
At this time, Liu Bi was twenty years old and followed Gaozu as a cavalry general to defeat Yingbu.
After King Liu Mai of Jing was killed by Ting Bu, he had no descendants.
Emperor Gaozu was worried that the people of Wu County and Kuaiji County were frivolous and fierce, and there were no powerful princes to control them. His son was still young, so he appointed Liu Bi as King of Wu in Pei, with jurisdiction over three counties and fifty-three cities.
After receiving the seal, Emperor Gaozu summoned him and gave him a face, saying: You have the appearance of rebellion.
I regretted that I should not have sealed him, but I could not take back my life, so I patted Liu Bi on the back and said: Fifty years after the founding of the Han Dynasty, there will be a rebellion in the southeast. Could it be you?

summary:
The histories of three Han dynasty princes: Liu Jia (King of Jing), Liu Ze (King of Yan), and Liu Bi (King of Wu). Liu Jia, a cousin of Emperor Gaozu, served as a general, leading campaigns against Chu forces, burning supplies, and later capturing Jiujiang. He was made King of Jing but was killed in battle against the rebel Yingbu. Liu Ze, a brother of Gaozu, was initially a marquis. Through a scheme by Tian Sheng, he was made King of Langye after persuading Empress Lu to enfeoff the Lü clan. After Empress Lu’s death, he joined others in supporting Emperor Xiaowen and was renamed King of Yan. His descendants included a son, Kang Wang, and a grandson, Dingguo, who committed adultery with his father’s concubine and sisters, leading to his suicide and the abolition of his state. Liu Bi, son of Liu Zhong, was made King of Wu after defeating Yingbu. He grew wealthy from copper and salt, and after his son was killed by the crown prince, he feigned illness and plotted rebellion. During Emperor Jing’s reign, Chao Cuo advocated reducing the princes’ territories, prompting Liu Bi to lead a rebellion of seven states (Wu, Chu, Zhao, Jiaoxi, Jiaodong, Jinan, and Zichuan) in 154 BCE. Liu Bi issued a call to arms, offering rewards for killing Han officials. Emperor Jing executed Chao Cuo to appease the rebels, but the rebellion continued. Zhou Yafu, a Han general, cut off Wu’s supply lines and defeated them. Liu Bi fled to Dongyue, where he was killed. The other rebel princes were also defeated or committed suicide. The text concludes that Liu Jia’s kingship was due to the unstable times, Liu Ze’s was based on a risky scheme, and Liu Bi’s rebellion stemmed from his wealth and personal grievance, leading to disaster.
