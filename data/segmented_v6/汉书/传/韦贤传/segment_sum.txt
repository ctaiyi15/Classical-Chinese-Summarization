summary:
The text then discusses the system of ancestral temples. During Emperor Gaozu's reign, temples were built in various vassal states. Over time, many temples were established. During Emperor Yuan's reign, Gong Yu proposed destroying temples that did not conform to ancient rituals. After discussion, many temples were abolished. Later, due to the emperor's illness and dreams, some temples were restored, but the ancestral temples in vassal states were ultimately abandoned. After Emperor Yuan's death, Kuang Heng proposed further abolitions. During Emperor Ai's reign, a debate occurred about whether to destroy Emperor Xiaowu's temple. Some argued for its preservation due to his great achievements, while others argued for its destruction based on ritual. The emperor adopted the suggestion to preserve it. During Emperor Ping's reign, Wang Mang proposed destroying the Huangkao Temple and other gardens, which was approved. The historian Ban Biao noted that the Han dynasty's ancestral system was determined according to the times, and the plans of the Confucian scholars, particularly Liu Xin, were profound.
