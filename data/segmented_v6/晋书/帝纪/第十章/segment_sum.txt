summary:
Emperor Gong, named Dewen, was Emperor An's half-brother. He had been enfeoffed as King of Langxie and held various positions including General of the Central Army and Grand Sima. After Huan Xuan's defeat, he returned to his former position. In 419, following Emperor An's death, Liu Yu issued a false edict proclaiming Emperor Gong's succession. In 420, following Liu Yu's instructions, Fu Liang persuaded Emperor Gong to abdicate. The emperor willingly complied, stating the Jin family had long lost the empire. He wrote the abdication edict on red paper. Liu Yu made him King of Lingling. In 422, Liu Yu sent soldiers who killed Emperor Gong in his inner chamber at age thirty-six. As a youth, Emperor Gong had been cruel and impetuous, once shooting horses for sport until someone noted that "Ma" (horse) was the imperial surname, making such killing unlucky. He then regretted his actions and became a devout Buddhist, casting a bronze statue and personally escorting it to Waguan Temple. He was known for his courtesy in serving his brother Emperor An, caring for his daily needs.
