summary:
Xiang Yu's forces were weakened by defections and food shortages. After a stalemate at Guangwu, Xiang Yu agreed to divide the empire at the Hong Canal, releasing the King of Han's family. However, the King of Han broke the treaty and pursued Xiang Yu. At Gaixia, Xiang Yu's army was surrounded, and hearing Chu songs, he realized defeat was imminent. He fled with 800 cavalry, but only 28 remained at Dongcheng. After killing hundreds of Han soldiers, he committed suicide, refusing to cross the Wu River. His body was divided among five Han officers for rewards. The city of Lu surrendered only after seeing his head, and he was buried with the rites of a duke. The Han king did not kill the Xiang clan, granting some titles. Sima Qian noted Xiang Yu's rise from obscurity to dominance in three years but criticized his arrogance, abandonment of Guanzhong, and failure to learn from the past, attributing his downfall to his own faults rather than divine will.
