The history of the Yan state, beginning with its founder, Zhao Gongxi, a member of the Zhou royal family granted the title in Northern Yan by King Wu. During King Cheng's reign, Zhao Gong governed west of Shaanxi, while the Duke of Zhou governed the east. Suspicious of the Duke of Zhou's regency, Zhao Gong was appeased by the Duke's historical references to virtuous Yin ministers. Zhao Gong governed the western region with great popularity, famously adjudicating under a Tangli tree, which the people later honored in the poem "Gantang" after his death. The succession of Yan rulers is then detailed, from Huihou through numerous generations, with key events interspersed: the flight of King Zhou Li, the rise of Qin as vassals, and the reign of King Xuan. The narrative covers Yan's interactions with other states, including attacks on King Hui of Zhou, invasions by Shan Rong (aided by Duke Huan of Qi), and participation in alliances against Qin. Internal strife emerges under King Kuai, who ceded power to his minister Zizhi, leading to chaos, Qi's invasion, and the death of King Kuai. King Zhao of Yan restored stability by recruiting talents like Le Yi, Zou Yan, and Ju Xin, leading to a prosperous period and a successful campaign against Qi, capturing its capital Linzi.
