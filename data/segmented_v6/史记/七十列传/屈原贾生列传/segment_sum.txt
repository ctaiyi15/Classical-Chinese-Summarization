Emperor Wen of the Han Dynasty also granted the title of Liehou to the four sons of King Li of Huainan.
Jia Yi advised him, believing that the rise of national disasters would start from here.
Jia Yi appealed to the emperor many times, saying that some princes had too many fiefdoms, even covering several counties, which was inconsistent with the ancient system, and their power should be gradually weakened, but Emperor Wen of Han refused to listen.
A few years later, King Liang Huai fell off his horse and died due to careless riding, leaving no descendants.
Jia Yi believed that he had failed to fulfill his responsibilities as a tutor. He was very sad and cried for more than a year before he died.
He was only thirty-three years old when he died.
Later, Emperor Wen of the Han Dynasty passed away and Emperor Wu of the Han Dynasty ascended the throne and promoted Jia Yi's two grandsons to serve as county guards. Among them, Jia Jia was the most eager to learn. He inherited Jia Yi's family business and had exchanges of letters with me.
By the time of Emperor Zhao of the Han Dynasty, he held the post of Jiuqing.

When I came to Changsha and visited the place where Qu Yuan drowned himself in the river, I couldn't help but shed tears and miss him as a person.
After reading Jia Sheng's poem in memory of Qu Yuan, I privately blamed Qu Yuan for his talent. If he went to lobby the princes, which country would not tolerate him? And actually put himself in such a situation!
After reading "Ode to the Birds", I realized that life and death were treated equally, and the ups and downs of one's life experience were not taken seriously. I couldn't help but feel at a loss.
