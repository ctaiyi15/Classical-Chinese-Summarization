It was more than a thousand years from Chunwei to Touman. The power of the Xiongnu grew and fell, and they often dispersed and differentiated. Because of the long time, their lineages cannot be arranged in sequence.
But when Maodun was the Chanyu, the Huns were the most powerful, making the northern barbarians completely submit to their rule and becoming an enemy of China in the south. After that, their lineage and national official titles could be recorded.
The Huns set up official positions such as the left and right virtuous kings, the left and right kings of Guli, the left and right generals, the left and right captains, the left and right Danghu, and the left and right Guduhou.
The Huns called virtuous people "Tu Qi", so they often made the prince the king of Zuo Tu Qi.

summary:
The historian Sima Qian notes that the Xiongnu's nomadic lifestyle and martial culture made them formidable adversaries. He criticizes Han officials for short-sighted policies and emphasizes the importance of selecting capable generals, as even wise rulers like Yao needed talented ministers to achieve success. The Xiongnu's lineage, from Chunwei to Modun, spanned over a thousand years, with their power fluctuating until Modun unified them. Their social structure, with hereditary titles and a focus on warfare, allowed them to dominate the northern steppes and challenge Chinese authority for centuries.
