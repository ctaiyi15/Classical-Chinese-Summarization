After Emperor Gaozu became emperor, he was deeply saddened and worried about this situation, so he issued an edict to search for knowledgeable scholars, rectify the five rituals, approve the six laws, revise the calendar, and correct the balance.
In the fourth year of Tianjian, Emperor Gaozu issued an edict saying: There are no wise men in the two deserts who have not taught Confucianism by virtue of their classics and skills, and have established a good reputation and achieved moral success.
In the Wei and Jin dynasties, the atmosphere was turbulent and Confucian learning declined. Perhaps because of this, the moral integrity could not be established.
I leave the court very late every day, just because I want to hear news about talented people. If you appoint talented people to get talented people, you should be rewarded.
Doctors on the Five Classics should be established, with one doctor for each classic, and schools should be established widely to accept young people to study.
So Pingyuan Mingshanbin, Wu Jun Lu Lian, Wu Xing Shen Jun, Jianping Yan Zhizhi, Kuaiji Hechang, etc. were appointed as replacement doctors, and each of them presided over a school.
There were hundreds of students in the academy, and the imperial court provided them with salaries.
Among them, those who choose things to report, if they are enlightened and wise, will be awarded as officials.
For more than ten years, people who were proficient in Confucian classics and well-read in Confucian classics gathered in the capital.
He also selected students to go to Yunmen Mountain in Kuaiji and study with He Yin in Lujiang.
Doctors were sent to each state and county to establish schools.
In the seventh year of Tianjian, Emperor Gaozu issued another edict: To establish a country and be the monarch of all people, establishing enlightenment and promoting education are the most important things; cultivating knowledge and sharpening virtues, classics study is the must way.
I have established a foundation that has spread throughout the world. Although I have worked hard to develop Confucianism, there are not many talented people, and the virtues are still lacking. It is not enough to cultivate the princes and nobles and make them obey the laws of Confucianism. I want to encourage them to respect their elders, practice it at home, and then become a model for the world.
Wherever the prestige is now, the Rong people in China are also inspired. They should vigorously open schools and widely recruit the children of the royal family and nobles to practice the ten ethics and promote the three virtues, so that the imperial education can be spread far and wide, and the exquisite words can be promoted.
So the crown princes, princes, royal families, and princes began to study in schools.
In vain, Emperor Gaozu condescended to go to the school in person to pay tribute to Zizi and other saints of the previous generation, talk to the students, encourage them, and comfort them with silk. The school was solemn and the students were numerous, and the journey of the Great Way reached such a prosperous level.
Among them, Fu Manrong, He Tongzhi and Fan Zhen were already famous in contemporary times; among the famous Confucian scholars at that time, Yan Zhizhi, He Yu and others were among the top candidates.
Now the deeds of these people are connected together and compiled into "The Scholars".
Fu Manrong, courtesy name Gongyi, was born in Anqiu, Pingchang.
His great-grandfather Fu Tao was a scholar in the Jin Dynasty.
His father Fu Yinzhi was the chief secretary of Sikong during the Song Dynasty.
Fu Manrong lost his father very early, and he lived in the South China Sea with his mother and brother.
Fu Manrong has been attentive and studious since he was a child, and he is proficient in "Laozi" and "Book of Changes". He is bold and free and likes to exaggerate. He once said: He Yan has questions about nine things in "Yi". From my perspective, He Yan does not study at all, so I know that Uncle He Ping has shortcomings.

summary:
Yao Cha noted that these scholars, despite varying fortunes, contributed to Confucian learning during the Liang Dynasty.
