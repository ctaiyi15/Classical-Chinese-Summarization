老子者，楚苦县厉乡曲仁里人也，姓李氏，名耳，字聃，周守藏室之史也。
孔子適周，将问礼於老子。
老子曰： 子所言者，其人与骨皆已朽矣，独其言在耳。
且君子得其时则驾，不得其时则蓬累而行。
吾闻之，良贾深藏若虚，君子盛德容貌若愚。
去子之骄气与多欲，态色与淫志，是皆无益於子之身。
吾所以告子，若是而已。
孔子去，谓弟子曰： 鸟，吾知其能飞；鱼，吾知其能游；兽，吾知其能走。
走者可以为罔，游者可以为纶，飞者可以为矰。
至於龙，吾不能知其乘风云而上天。
吾今日见老子，其犹龙邪！
老子脩道德，其学以自隐无名为务。
居周久之，见周之衰，乃遂去。
至关，关令尹喜曰： 子将隐矣，彊为我著书。
於是老子乃著书上下篇，言道德之意五千馀言而去，莫知其所终。
或曰：老莱子亦楚人也，著书十五篇，言道家之用，与孔子同时云。
盖老子百有六十馀岁，或言二百馀岁，以其脩道而养寿也。
自孔子死之後百二十九年，而史记周太史儋见秦献公曰： 始秦与周合，合五百岁而离，离七十岁而霸王者出焉。
或曰儋即老子，或曰非也，世莫知其然否。
老子，隐君子也。
老子之子名宗，宗为魏将，封於段干。
宗子注，注子宫，宫玄孙假，假仕於汉孝文帝。
而假之子解为胶西王卬太傅，因家于齐焉。
世之学老子者则绌儒学，儒学亦绌老子。 道不同不相为谋 ，岂谓是邪？
李耳无为自化，清静自正。

I am a native of Qurenli, Li Township, Kuxian County, Chu State. His surname was Li, his given name was Er, and his courtesy name was Dan. He was a historian in charge of the library in the Zhou Dynasty.
Confucius went to the capital of Zhou and wanted to ask Laozi about etiquette.
Lao Tzu said: The etiquette you are talking about, the person and the bones of the person who advocated it have rotted away, only his words remain.
Moreover, a gentleman will drive his car and go out to be an official when his fortune comes. If he is born at the wrong time, he will be blown away by the wind like a fluffy grass.
I have heard that people who are good at business hide their goods as if they were nothing. A gentleman has a noble character, but his appearance is as humble as a dull person.
Abandon your arrogance and excessive desires, abandon your artificial moods and unrealistic ambitions, these are not good for yourself.
That's all I can tell you.
After Confucius left, he said to his disciples: I know birds are good at flying; fish, I know they are good at swimming; animals, I know they are good at running.
Those that can run can weave a net to catch it, those that can swim can be made into silk thread to catch it, and those that can fly can shoot it with arrows.
As for the dragon, I don't know what to do. It is said that it rides the wind and soars into the sky.
The Lao Tzu I saw today probably looks like a dragon!
Lao Tzu studied moral knowledge, and his teachings aimed at concealing traces and not seeking to be heard.
He lived in Zhoudu for a long time. Seeing that the Zhou Dynasty was declining, he left Zhoudu.
When he arrived at Hangu Pass, Guan Ling Yinxi said to him: You are going to live in seclusion and try your best to write a book for us.
So I wrote a book, divided into two parts, expounding the original meaning of morality, with a total of more than 5,000 words. Then I left. No one knows where he went in the end.
Some people say: Lao Laizi was also from the Chu state. He wrote fifteen books on how to apply Taoist knowledge. He was a contemporary of Confucius.
It is said that I lived for more than 160 years, and some people say that I lived for more than 200 years. This is because he can cultivate his mind and live a long life.
One hundred and twenty-nine years after the death of Confucius, historical records record that when Taishi Dan of Zhou met with Duke Xiang of Qin, he prophesied: When the Qin State and the Zhou Dynasty were united, they were united for five hundred years and then separated. After seventy years of separation, there will be people who will become kings and hegemons.
Some people say that Taishi Dan is Lao Tzu, and some people say that he is not. No one in the world knows which statement is correct.
In short, I am a true hidden gentleman.
Lao Tzu's son is called Li Zong. He served as a general of Wei State and his fiefdom was in Duanqian.
Li Zong's son is called Li Zhu, Li Zhu's son is called Li Gong, and Li Gong's great-great-grandson is called Li Jie. Li Jie served as an official during the reign of Emperor Wen of Han Dynasty.
Li Jie's son Li Jie served as the Taifu of Jiaoxi King Liu Xuan, so the Li family settled in Qidi.
Anyone in the world who studies Laozi's knowledge will disparage Confucian thought and doctrine; and those who study Confucianism will also disparage Laozi's doctrine. People with different opinions cannot agree with each other. Is this the case?
Lao Tzu believes that if you govern by doing nothing, the people will naturally become corrupt; if you are quiet and do nothing, the people will naturally return to righteousness.

summary:
A biographical account of Laozi, a native of Chu State who served as a Zhou dynasty librarian. When Confucius sought his advice on etiquette, Laozi dismissed the topic, noting that its proponents had long since perished, and advised Confucius to abandon arrogance and excessive desires. Confucius later likened Laozi to a dragon, elusive and transcendent. Laozi, observing Zhou's decline, wrote a book of over 5,000 words on morality at the request of a gatekeeper before disappearing. Some claim he lived over 160 years, while others identify him with the historian Taishi Dan, who prophesied about Qin and Zhou's future. Laozi's son, Li Zong, became a Wei general, and his descendants served in Han times. The text contrasts Laozi's philosophy of non-action with Confucianism, noting mutual criticism between followers.
