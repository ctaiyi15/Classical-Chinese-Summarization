魏王释博，欲召大臣谋。
公子止王曰： 赵王田猎耳，非为寇也。
复博如故。
王恐，心不在博。
居顷，复从北方来传言曰： 赵王猎耳，非为寇也。
魏王大惊，曰： 公子何以知之？
公子曰： 臣之客有能深得赵王阴事者，赵王所为，客辄以报臣，臣以此知之。
是後魏王畏公子之贤能，不敢任公子以国政。
魏有隐士曰侯嬴，年七十，家贫，为大梁夷门监者。

公子於是乃置酒大会宾客。
坐定，公子从车骑，虚左，自迎夷门侯生。
侯生摄敝衣冠，直上载公子上坐，不让，欲以观公子。
公子执辔愈恭。
侯生又谓公子曰： 臣有客在市屠中，原枉车骑过之。
公子引车入巿，侯生下见其客朱亥，俾倪故久立，与其客语，微察公子。
公子颜色愈和。
当是时，魏将相宗室宾客满堂，待公子举酒。

translation:
Mr. Wei's name is Wuji, he is the youngest son of King Wei Zhao and the half-brother of King Wei Anli.
After the death of King Zhao, King Anli came to the throne and named the son Lord Xinling.
At that time, Fan Sui fled from Wei to Qin and served as Qin Prime Minister. Because he was resentful that Wei Qi Qu beat him almost to death, he sent Qin troops to besiege Daliang and defeated the Wei troops stationed in Huayang, causing Wei general Mang Mao to flee in defeat.
The king of Wei and his son were very anxious about this matter.
The prince was kind, generous, courteous and virtuous. Regardless of whether the scholars had talent or talent, he interacted with them courteously. He never dared to look down on the scholars because of his wealth.
Therefore, scholars from thousands of miles around rushed to join him, and he attracted 3,000 diners.
At that time, the princes and states did not dare to invade Wei for more than ten years because of the virtuous son and the large number of guests.
One time, the young master and the King of Wei were playing chess, and an alarm came from the northern border, saying that Zhao State was sending troops to invade and would enter the border.
The king of Wei immediately put down his chess pieces and summoned the ministers to discuss countermeasures.
The prince dissuaded the king of Wei and said: "The king of Zhao is just hunting, not invading the border."
Then he continued to play chess with King Wei as if nothing had happened.
But the king of Wei was frightened and did not focus on playing chess at all.
After a while, news came from the north saying: King Zhao is just hunting, not invading the border.
King Wei was greatly surprised when he heard this and asked: How did the young master know this?
The young master replied: One of my diners can go deep into the secrets of King Zhao. If King Zhao takes any action, he will report it to me immediately, so I know about it.
From then on, the king of Wei was afraid of the young master's talents and did not dare to use him to handle national affairs.
There was a hermit named Hou Ying in the state of Wei. He was seventy years old and came from a poor family. He was the gatekeeper of the east gate of Daliang City.
When the prince heard about this man, he sent someone to see him and wanted to give him a generous gift.
But Hou Ying refused to accept it, saying: I have cultivated my moral character and insisted on integrity for decades, but after all, I cannot accept the prince's financial gift because I am a poor janitor.
The young master then held a banquet and entertained the guests.
After everyone came and sat down, the young master took the carriage, horses and entourage, vacated the left seat in the carriage, and went to the east gate in person to greet Mr. Hou.
Mr. Hou tidied up his shabby clothes and hats, then got into the car and sat in the distinguished seat vacated by the young master. He had no intention of being humble at all, and wanted to observe the young master's attitude.
But the young master held the horse's reins more respectfully.
Mr. Hou said to the young master again: I have a friend who is in the slaughterhouse in the market. I would like to give him a ride and take me to visit him.
The young master immediately drove to the market. Mr. Hou got out of the car and went to meet his friend Zhu Hai. He squinted at the young master and deliberately stood there for a long time, chatting with his friend and secretly observing the young master.
The young master's face became more cheerful.
At this time, the generals, prime ministers, clan ministers and distinguished guests from Wei State were all sitting in the hall, waiting for the young master to raise their glasses to start the banquet.

summary:
Lord Xinling, named Wei Wuji, was the youngest son of King Wei Zhao and half-brother of King Wei Anli. After King Zhao's death, King Anli enfeoffed him as Lord Xinling. Fan Sui, who fled to Qin and became prime minister, resented Wei's mistreatment and led Qin forces to besiege Daliang, defeating Wei troops at Huayang. The king and lord were deeply troubled.
