Dong Zhuo appointed him general, but others warned of Zheng's cunning, so Dong Zhuo revoked the command. Zheng later plotted to kill Dong Zhuo, but the plan leaked. **Xun Yu**, grandson of Magistrate Xun Shu, was recognized as extraordinary by He Yong. Recommended as Xiaolian, he abandoned office when Dong Zhuo rebelled. He warned his clan to flee Yingchuan, predicting war; those who stayed were later killed. He joined Yuan Shao but, seeing the Han's chaos, left for Cao Cao, who called him "my Zhang Liang." When Zhang Miao and Chen Gong rebelled, Xun Yu defended Zhen City, outsmarting their deception and personally meeting Guo Gong to prevent his alliance with rebels. He secured three cities for Cao Cao's return. Xun Yu advised Cao Cao to stabilize Yanzhou before attacking Xuzhou, leading to Lu Bu's defeat. He urged Cao Cao to welcome Emperor Xian to Xuchang, arguing this would bring legitimacy. Xun Yu recommended talented men like Xun You, Zhong Yao, and Guo Jia. He predicted Yuan Shao's defeat despite his strength, correctly assessing his generals' weaknesses. During the Guandu stalemate, when Cao Cao wanted to retreat, Xun Yu insisted on holding position, leading to Yuan Shao's defeat. He advised against attacking Liu Biao before pacifying Yuan Shao's remnants. When Cao Cao wanted to restore the ancient Jiuzhou system, Xun Yu warned it would cause panic, and Cao Cao abandoned the plan. Cao Cao praised Xun Yu's strategies as turning defeat into victory. When Dong Zhao proposed making Cao Cao Duke of Jin, Xun Yu opposed it, saying Cao Cao should remain loyal to Han. Cao Cao grew resentful. During a southern campaign, Cao Cao kept Xun Yu at his camp, then sent him an empty food box. Xun Yu took poison and died at fifty. The next year, Cao Cao declared himself Duke of Wei.
