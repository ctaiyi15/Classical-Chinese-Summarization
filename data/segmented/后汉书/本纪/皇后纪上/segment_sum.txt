summary line 0: Before the Xia and Shang dynasties, records of the concubine system were sparse. "The Rites of Zhou" describes the emperor's harem hierarchy: a queen, three wives, nine concubines, twenty-seven wives, and eighty-one female concubines, each with specific duties. The queen held equal status to the emperor, while wives assisted her in women's ethics. Concubines taught women's virtues, speech, appearance, and merits. Female officials managed rituals, dining, and sleeping arrangements, with a female historian recording merits and demerits. This system aimed to maintain order and prevent corruption, as seen in Queen Kang of Zhou's virtuous conduct. However, after the Zhou capital moved to Luoyang, etiquette declined. Dukes like Huan of Qi and Xian of Jin indulged in multiple wives, leading to succession conflicts and state ruin. Historians criticize her for holding power too long, leading to resentment, but acknowledge her efforts to stabilize the state.
