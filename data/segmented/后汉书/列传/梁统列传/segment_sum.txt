summary:
summary line 1: In the fifth year of Jianwu's reign, Liang Tong and others sent envoys to the imperial court with Dou Rong's chief minister Liu Jun to pay tribute. The emperor conferred on Liang Tong the title of General Xuande. In the eighth year, Emperor Guangwu personally conquered Kui Xiao, and Liang Tong and Dou Rong led soldiers to join him. After Kui Xiao's defeat, Guangwu granted Liang Tong the title of Marquis of Chengyi, and his brother Liang Xun and cousin Liang Teng were also made marquises. In the twelfth year, Liang Tong and Dou Rong came to the capital as marquises. Liang Tong was granted the title of Gaoshan Marquis, appointed Taizhong大夫, and his four sons were named Lang. While in court, Liang Tong repeatedly reported beneficial matters. He believed the law was too light, increasing crime, and advocated for stricter punishments according to old laws. He submitted a memorial arguing that lenient punishments under Emperors Yuan and Ai had led to lawlessness and increased crime. He cited historical precedents from the Five Emperors, Three Kings, and Han emperors, urging the adoption of stricter measures. However, the matter was referred to the Sangong and Tingwei, who deemed harsh punishments unnecessary and refused to change the old law. Liang Tong wrote again, clarifying he did not seek severe punishment but adherence to old rules. He argued that mitigating punishment had caused rampant theft and rebellion, citing recent outbreaks of banditry. His memorial was sent but not reported. Later, Liang Tong served as Prefect of Jiujiang and was granted the title of Marquis of Lingxiang. He achieved great governance in the county, earning the fear and love of officials and people, and died in office.
