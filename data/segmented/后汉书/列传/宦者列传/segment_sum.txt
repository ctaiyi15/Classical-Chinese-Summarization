Initially, they were employed for their perceived lack of evil thoughts and suitability for managing the inner palace. However, over time, their roles expanded. While some, like Bo Diao and Guan Su, made contributions to states, others caused disasters, such as in Qi and Song. During the Han Dynasty, the system continued, with eunuchs like Zhang Qing serving Empress Lü and later emperors. Their power grew significantly during the Eastern Han, starting with Zheng Zhong, who helped Emperor He eliminate the Dou Xian faction and was enfeoffed, marking the beginning of eunuch influence. This trend continued under subsequent rulers, with eunuchs like Sun Cheng playing key roles in supporting emperors, leading to their increased authority and the granting of titles and lands.

Emperor Wu had banquets in the harem many times, or secretly visited the villa outside the palace. Therefore, eunuchs were usually responsible for reporting confidential matters.
By the time of Emperor Yuan, Shi You was the magistrate of Huangmen. He was loyal and diligent, and was very helpful to political affairs.
Later, Hong Gong and Shi Xian relied on insidious flattery to get promoted, which finally led to the murder of Xiao Wangzhi and the deposed Zhou Kan, which damaged and tarnished the emperor's virtue.
At the beginning of the Eastern Han Dynasty when the dynasty was reestablished, all eunuchs were eunuchs and no longer mixed with other scholars.
During the Yongping period, the staff began to be recruited: four people from the Zhongchang sect and ten people from the Xiaohuang sect.
Emperor He ascended the throne at a young age, and the Dou Xian brothers controlled the government. Officials inside and outside were unable to approach Emperor He, and only eunuchs lived with Emperor He.
Therefore, the eunuch Zheng Zhong was able to make plans with Emperor He alone in the forbidden palace, and finally killed the traitorous minister Dou Xian. Zheng Zhong was named the Marquis of Chao'an Township and was given the title of Palace Minister.
The power of eunuchs began to grow.
From Emperor Ming to the Yanping period, the scope of eunuch appointments was gradually expanded, and the number of eunuchs was slightly increased. The number of eunuchs increased to ten, and the number of small Huangmen was twenty. The decorations on their hats were all changed to gold right mink, and they also served as Jiuqing and other foreign court officials.
Empress Dowager Deng presided over the government as a heroine, and there were many political affairs. The courtiers discussed national affairs and could not enter the inner palace to participate in planning and issue edicts without going beyond the queen's residence. Therefore, she had to appoint eunuchs and pin the fate of the country on them.
They hold the power of nobility in their hands and carry the royal decrees in their mouths. They no longer just hold positions in the palace and Yongxiang.
Later, the eunuch Sun Cheng made great achievements in supporting Emperor Shun, and Cao Teng participated in the plan to support Emperor Huan. Subsequently, the five marquises Shan Chao, Xu Huang, Ju Yuan, Zuo Xuanguan, and Tang Heng conspired to have Liang Ji, a relative of his, executed. What they did was fair and just, and their kindness was in the emperor's heart, so they obeyed both inside and outside the court, and no one dared to criticize them.
Some people praise them for having the same achievements as Yi Yin and Huo Guang, and are worthy of the previous generations; some say that the strategies of Zhang Liang and Chen Ping have appeared again today.
Although there were loyal ministers who served the public affairs at that time, they were eventually rejected.

summary:
summary line 5: The historian concludes that the fall of the Eastern Han was due to the gradual accumulation of eunuch power, which corrupted the court and alienated the people. While some eunuchs were loyal and capable, the majority were self-serving and destructive. Their influence, combined with the weakness of young emperors and the machinations of female regents, led to the dynasty’s collapse. The historian emphasizes that the eunuchs’ ability to gain trust and manipulate the court, coupled with their lack of familial ties, made them particularly dangerous. The decline was not sudden but a slow process, as warned in the *Book of Changes*: "Walking on frost, solid ice will come."
