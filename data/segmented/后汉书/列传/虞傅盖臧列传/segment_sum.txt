summary:
summary line 0: Yu Xu, from Wuping in Chen, was known for his integrity. His grandfather, a prison officer, handled cases with compassion. Yu Xu was orphaned young and cared for his grandmother, refusing an official post until after her death. He later served as a doctor. When General Deng Zhi proposed abandoning Liangzhou due to Qiang rebellions, Yu Xu argued against it, stating it would expose the imperial tombs and that Liangzhou's people were a vital defense. He suggested controlling local leaders by giving their sons official posts to prevent rebellion. Deng Zhi, resentful of Yu Xu's opposition, sent him to govern Chaoge, a bandit-ridden area. Yu Xu used deception, recruiting former bandits to ambush others and marking thieves' clothes with silk threads, leading to their dispersal. Promoted to Wudu's prefect, he again used deception against the Qiang, increasing his campfires to feign strength and marching rapidly. He then used a strategy of weak arrows to lure the Qiang into range of his strong crossbows, defeating them. He improved the county by building fortifications, opening waterways for transport, and increasing the population. As Sili Xiaowei, he impeached powerful officials, earning their hatred. He was framed and sentenced to hard labor, but eunuch Sun Cheng defended him, leading to his release and promotion. He later abolished the practice of "righteous money" and argued for fair treatment of petitioners. He remained upright despite nine reprimands and three punishments, dying as Shangshu Ling. On his deathbed, he regretted killing many bandits at Chaoge, believing it brought divine punishment.
