summary:
summary line 0: The historical and cultural evolution of the Eastern Yi peoples, beginning with their origins and characteristics. The Yi, meaning "root," were considered docile and easily governed by morality, leading to the emergence of a "country of gentlemen" and a "country of immortality." They were divided into nine branches, and Confucius once expressed a desire to live among them. Early interactions with Chinese dynasties included Yao sending Xizhong to Woyi and the Yi submitting to Xia rulers, though rebellions occurred under tyrants like Jie. Over centuries, the Yi fluctuated between submission and revolt, with notable events such as Xu Yi usurping the title of emperor and attacking Zongzhou, only to be defeated by King Wen of Chu. Later, during the Zhou and Han dynasties, the Yi peoples, including Huaiyi and Siyi, were scattered, conquered, or assimilated, with periods of tribute and conflict under rulers like Wang Mang and Emperor Wu of Han.
