summary:
summary line 2: Guo Ji, from Maoling, Fufeng, was born into a family with a knightly ancestor and a prefect father. During the reigns of Emperor Ai and Emperor Ping, he served in the Da Sikong Mansion and became Yuyang Duwei. Under Wang Mang, he was Great Yin of Shanggu. During the Gengshi era, when the three auxiliaries were invaded, Gengshi appointed him Zuo Fengyi to pacify the people. After Shizu ascended, Guo Ji became Yongzhou's pastor and later a minister offering loyal advice. In the fourth year of Jianwu, he became prefect of Zhongshan, then governor of Yuyang after Peng Chong's defeat. There, he eliminated bandits through punishment and reward, and organized defenses against the Xiongnu, who ceased border incursions. After five years, household registrations doubled. In the ninth year, he became prefect of Yingchuan to suppress bandits, successfully recruiting Zhao Hong and Zhao Wu to surrender. Later appointed Bingzhou pastor, he advised selecting officials from worthy candidates nationwide rather than exclusively from Nanyang. He was known for keeping promises to children who welcomed him on horseback. He served long in Bingzhou due to Lu Fang's troubles, eventually forcing Lu Fang to flee to the Xiongnu. He resigned due to age and illness, became Taizhong doctor, distributed his wealth among his clan, and died at eighty-six.

Feeling uneasy about his merit, he requested demotion, arguing that troops needed rest and that meritorious generals should be rewarded with county positions. The emperor refused. Du Shi recommended talented people and proposed using tiger talismans for troop mobilization to prevent forgery, which the emperor accepted. After seven years, he was recalled due to a case involving his brother's revenge. He died poor without land or house; the emperor provided funeral expenses.
