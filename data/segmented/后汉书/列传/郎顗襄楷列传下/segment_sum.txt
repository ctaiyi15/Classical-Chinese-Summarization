◆The Legend of Lang Yi
Lang Yi, courtesy name Yaguang, was born in Anqiu, Beihai.
His father, Langzong, whose courtesy name was Zhongsui, studied "Jing's Book of Changes" and was good at wind angle, astrology, and six-day and seven-minute calculations. He could tell the weather about good and bad luck, and he often sold divination to support himself.
Emperor An summoned him, and his countermeasures set an example for other Confucian scholars. Later, he became the magistrate of Wu County.
At this time, there was a sudden storm, and Lang Zong learned from divination that there was going to be a fire in the capital. He noted the time and sent someone to inquire, and it was indeed as he said.
After hearing this, all the princes went up to the table to hear the news, and the imperial court summoned him with a doctor.
Lang Zong thought it was shameful to be known for his fortune-telling. When he heard that the summons had arrived, he hung his seal and ribbon in the county court at night and fled, so he never served as an official for the rest of his life.
When he was young, Lang Yi passed on his father's studies and learned the classics. He lived in seclusion by the sea and often taught hundreds of disciples.
During the day, he studies the essence and at night, he divines the images. He meditates diligently and is tireless in the morning and evening.
The prefectures and counties appointed him to be a man of integrity and uprightness, but he did not go to the post.
During the reign of Emperor Shun, disasters occurred many times. In the first month of the second year of Yangjia, when public transport was called for, Lang Yi went to the court to pay homage and wrote a memorial: I heard that monsters descended from the sky and disaster talismans appeared on the earth, in order to condemn the masters, to punish the people, to cultivate virtues, to balance the good opportunities, and to promote the government.
