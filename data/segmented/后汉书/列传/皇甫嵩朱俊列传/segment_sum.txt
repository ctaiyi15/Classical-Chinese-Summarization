summary:

From a young age, Huangfu Song was learned in the *Classic of Poetry* and *Book of Documents*, and skilled in archery, swordsmanship, and horsemanship. summary line 1: When Zhang Jiao founded the Way of Great Peace, claiming to cure illness through faith and talismanic water, he amassed hundreds of thousands of followers across eight provinces over a decade. He organized them into thirty-six "squares," each led by a general, and spread the prophecy: "The Azure Heaven is dead; the Yellow Heaven shall rise. In the year of Jiazi, the world will be blessed." translation: 
In the spring of the next year, the imperial court sent an envoy to appoint Zhu Jun as the Right Cavalry General on the Emperor's Day. He returned to the capital in triumph and was appointed Guanglu Doctor.
He resigned from the official position due to the death of his mother, and later came out from home to serve as an official. He was appointed as a master craftsman, and transferred to the Shaofu and Tai servants.
Since the Yellow Turban rebellion, there have been countless followers from Heishan, Huanglong, Baibo, Zuoxiao, Guo Daxian, Yu Digen, Qingniujiao, Zhang Baiqi, Liu Shi, Zuo Zhuzhangba, Pinghan, Daji, Sili, Juzai, Lei Gong, Fuyun, Feiyan, Baique, Yangfeng, Yudu, Wulu, Li Damu, Bairao, Qigu, and Kuca, and they have appeared in the valley.
Among them, the loud one is called Lei Gong, the one riding a white horse is called Zhang Baiqi, the brisk one is called Feiyan, the one with many beards is called Yu Digen, and the one with big eyes is called Damu. Each of these titles has its own reasons.
The big one has 20,000 to 30,000 people, and the small one has 6,000 to 7,000 people.
Zhang Yan, a bandit commander from Changshan, was light, brave and good at walking, so he was called Feiyan in the army.
He was very loved by the soldiers, and interacted with bandits in Zhongshan, Changshan, Zhaojun, Shangdang, and Hanoi valleys. Their number reached one million, and they were called Black Mountain Bandits.
All counties and counties in Hebei suffered from them, and the imperial court could not attack them.
Zhang Yan sent an envoy to the capital to beg for surrender. The imperial court awarded Zhang Yan the rank of Lieutenant General in times of difficulty, allowing him to manage affairs in the valleys of Hebei. He could be promoted to filial piety, honesty, and planning officials every year.
Zhang Yan gradually invaded Hanoi and approached the capital, so he appointed Zhu Jun as the governor of Hanoi and led his troops to repel him.
After that, many of the thieves were put down by Yuan Shao, which was recorded in the "Biography of Yuan Shao". Zhu Jun was again appointed as the Guanglu doctor, and he was assigned to the garrison and cavalry. Soon, he was awarded the title of Chengmen Xiaowei and Henan Yin.
