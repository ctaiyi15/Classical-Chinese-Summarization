summary:
summary line 0: Li Tong, courtesy name Ciyuan, was from Wan County, Nanyang. His family was renowned for business, and his father Li Shou was a tall, imposing man who served Wang Mang. Li Tong worked for General Wuwei and later became magistrate of Wu County, known for his competence. During Wang Mang's late reign, Li Tong heard his father's prophecy about the Liu family's revival with the Li family as assistants. Wealthy and disinclined to serve, he resigned and returned home. When rebellions erupted in Nanyang, Li Tong and his cousin Li Yi discussed joining Liu Boxheng (Liu Yan) and his brother Guangwu (Liu Xiu) to restore the Han. Li Tong sent Li Yi to greet Guangwu, and they formed a pact. They planned to kidnap officials Zhen Fu and Liang Qiuci to spark an uprising. Li Tong sent his cousin's son Li Ji to inform Li Shou in Chang'an, but Li Ji died en route. Li Shou, learning the secret, tried to flee but was advised by Huang Xian to request leave. The plot was discovered; Wang Mang arrested Li Shou and, despite Huang Xian's pleas, executed both and Li Shou's family in Chang'an. Nanyang also killed sixty-four of Li Tong's clan members. Li Tong joined Guangwu and Li Yi in Jiyang, attacking and killing Zhen Fu and Liang Qiuci. Under Gengshi, Li Tong became General of Zhuguo and later King of Xiping. He married Guangwu's sister, Princess Ningping. After Guangwu ascended, Li Tong served as Grand Minister of Agriculture, guarding the capital during campaigns. In Jianwu 5-6, he led troops against Hanzhong bandits and Gongsun Shu, defeating them. As peace prevailed, Li Tong sought to retire due to illness, but Guangwu refused. He became Grand Sikong but remained humble, often taking sick leave. Guangwu favored him, granting his son Li Xiong a marquisate. Li Tong died in Jianwu 18, posthumously titled Gonghou. His lineage continued, and he alone among his kin lived with honor.
