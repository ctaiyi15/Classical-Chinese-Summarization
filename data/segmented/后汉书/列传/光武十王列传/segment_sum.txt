◆In the second year of Jianwu's reign, Prince Gong of Donghai, Liu Qiang, made his mother Guo the queen and Liu Qiang the crown prince.

When Liu Qiang arrived at the fiefdom, he wrote several times to return the East China Sea, and asked the crown prince to resolutely resign.

At first, King Lu Gong liked to build palaces and built the Lingguang Palace, which was very magnificent and still existed at this time. Therefore, the emperor issued an edict to let Liu Qiang make Lu his capital.
In the first year of the Zhongyuan Dynasty, he entered the imperial court and followed the emperor to grant Zen to Mount Tai, so he stayed in the capital.
In the spring of the next year, the emperor died.
In winter, Liu Qiang returned to his fief.
In the first year of Yongping, Liu Qiang fell ill. Chang Shidun of the Xian sect ordered the imperial physician to take a post car to see a doctor. He ordered King Liu Fu of Pei, King Liu Kang of Jinan, and King Liu Yan of Huaiyang to wait for Lu.
When Liu Qiang died, he thanked him on his deathbed and said: "I have been blessed by God to be a supporter of Tibet. I have received special support from two countries. I have been given the courtesy and music in the palace. Everything is different. My great virtues are immeasurable, but I have never repaid you."
However, he was careless in self-cultivation, fell ill for many years, and was worried about by the court.

summary:
summary line 0: Emperor Guangwu had eleven sons. Queen Guo bore him several sons, including Liu Qiang, who was initially made crown prince. In the 17th year of Jianwu, Empress Guo was deposed, causing Liu Qiang great anxiety. He repeatedly expressed his wish to step down as crown prince and become a vassal king. Emperor Guangwu eventually agreed in the 19th year, naming him King of Donghai. The emperor, believing Liu Qiang was not at fault, granted him an exceptionally large fief of 29 counties, along with special honors like tiger-head talismans and palace bells and drums. Even after arriving at his fief, Liu Qiang repeatedly tried to return lands, but the emperor refused. He was later allowed to make Lu his capital. Liu Qiang died in the 1st year of Yongping. On his deathbed, he wrote a grateful and humble letter to the emperor, expressing his unworthiness of the honors and his concern for his son. The emperor was deeply saddened and ordered a grand funeral with special rites, attended by many nobles. To honor Liu Qiang's modesty, the emperor decreed a simple burial.
