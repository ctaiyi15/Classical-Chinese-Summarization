Resolute and generous, he was angered by Wang Mang's usurpation of the Han dynasty and used his family wealth to befriend heroes, planning to restore the Han. During the chaos of Wang Mang's final years, Bosheng gathered heroes and raised troops with relatives and guests, including Deng Chen in Xinye and Guangwu, Li Tong, and Li Yi in Wan. He mobilized 7,000–8,000 disciples from Chongling, calling them the Zhutiandu tribe. After allying with Xinshi and Pinglin soldiers, they captured several locations and attacked Wan. At Xiaochang'an, they were defeated by Wang Mang's officers Zhen Fu and Liangqiu Ci in dense fog, resulting in the deaths of Bosheng's sister Liu Yuan and brother Liu Zhong. Bosheng regrouped at Jiyang. The enemy forces, with 100,000 elite troops, camped between the Huangchun and Pubi Rivers, removing their bridge to show no retreat. Xinshi and Pinglin soldiers wanted to disperse, but Bosheng convinced 5,000 arriving Xiajiang soldiers to join forces. After a three-day rest, they secretly attacked and captured Lanxiang, seizing enemy baggage. The next morning, the Han army attacked from the southwest and Xiajiang from the southeast, defeating Liangqiu Ci's army. Zhenfu's army fled but was blocked by the Huangchun River; over 20,000 were killed or drowned, and Zhenfu and Liangqiu Ci were beheaded. Bosheng then defeated Yan You and Chen Mao at Yuyang, beheading over 3,000, and besieged Wancheng, naming himself General Zhutian. Wang Mang offered a huge reward for Bosheng's death and had his image painted for archery practice. Over 100,000 people surrendered daily. When generals discussed establishing a Liu clan leader, they favored Bosheng but feared his strictness and preferred Sheng Gong (Gengshi), whom they established. Bosheng warned against premature enthronement, citing Chen Sheng and Xiang Ji as examples, and suggested waiting until after defeating Wang Mang and dealing with Chimei.
