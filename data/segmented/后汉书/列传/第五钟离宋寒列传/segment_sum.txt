summary:
summary line 0: Fifth Lun, courtesy name Boyu, was from Changling, Jingzhao, descended from the Tian clan of Qi. Upright from youth, he built fortresses during Wang Mang's era to defend against bandits like Bronze Horse and Red Eyebrow, successfully repelling attacks. He served under Yin Xianyu Bao, who recognized his talent, and later became a village official, gaining popularity through fair tax collection and dispute mediation. He moved to Hedong, changed his name to Wang Boqi, and worked as a salt carrier. Recommended by Xianyu Bao, he became chief bookkeeper under Jingzhao Yin Yanxing, where he rectified market scales and coins, earning public trust. Promoted to Xiaolian in 51 CE, he served as chief medical officer for Huaiyang State. Emperor Guangwu, impressed by his governance insights, appointed him governor of Fuyi, then Kuaiji. In Kuaiji, he banned superstitious cattle sacrifices and witch fraud, restoring order. Transferred in 62 CE for legal violations, he was pursued by weeping locals. After release, he farmed privately until appointed magistrate of Danqu County, then governor of Shu Commandery, where he dismissed corrupt officials and promoted virtuous ones, ending bribery. As Sikong under Suzong, he warned against the power of Empress Dowager Mingde's Ma relatives, citing their lavish gifts and potential corruption. He also opposed Ma Fang's Western Qiang campaign, fearing nepotism. Known for strictness but leniency, he criticized harsh governance, advocating for benevolent officials. He retired in 86 CE with honors and died over eighty, receiving imperial burial gifts.
