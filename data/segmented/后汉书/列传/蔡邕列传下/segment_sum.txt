His sixth-generation ancestor Cai Xun served under Emperor Ping but refused to serve Wang Mang, fleeing into the mountains with his family to maintain loyalty to Han. summary:
summary line 2: Cai Yong submitted a memorial criticizing the "three mutual laws" that prevented officials from serving in related regions, arguing they hindered governance in You and Ji provinces. He urged their abolition to select talented officials promptly, but his advice was not adopted. He also addressed disasters and Xianbei invasions, proposing seven reforms: proper imperial sacrifices, recruiting virtuous scholars, rewarding loyal officials like Zhang Wen, empowering honest governors, and ending corrupt recruitment practices. His memorial was leaked by Cao Jie, leading to false accusations from enemies like Yang Qiu. Cai Yong was imprisoned and sentenced to death, but Lu Qiang interceded, and his sentence was commuted to exile in Shuofang. Despite assassination attempts by Yang Qiu, he survived.

He lived in Wu for twelve years, once crafting a famous "Jiaowei Qin" from a burnt tung tree. In the sixth year of Zhongping, Dong Zhuo forced him into service, promoting him rapidly. Cai Yong advised Dong Zhuo against excessive honors but grew disillusioned with his tyranny. After Dong Zhuo's execution, Cai Yong expressed regret, angering Wang Yun, who imprisoned him. Despite pleas from scholars like Ma Rishidan, Wang Yun executed him, fearing his historical writings would slander the court. Cai Yong died at sixty-one, mourned by many.
