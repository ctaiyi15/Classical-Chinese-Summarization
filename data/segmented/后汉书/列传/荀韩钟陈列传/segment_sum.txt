summary line 1: **Xun Shu (Jihe)** was a native of Yingyin, Yingchuan County, and the eleventh-generation grandson of Xun Qing. From youth, he possessed noble virtues and profound knowledge, though he disliked detailed textual analysis and was looked down upon by vulgar scholars. During Emperor An's reign, he was recruited as a doctor and later promoted to Tu Chang, but resigned to return home. The famous sages Li Gu and Li Ying respected him as their teacher. When Empress Dowager Liang governed, Xun Shu was promoted to answer imperial questions, where he criticized the emperor's noble relatives and favored people. General Liang Ji, jealous of him, transferred him to serve as Marquis of Langling. Known for his upright governance, he was called the "divine king." He soon resigned and returned home, distributing family property among clan members. He died at sixty-seven in the third year of Jianhe. Li Ying mourned him for three years. Both counties built temples in his honor. Xun Shu had eight sons—Jian, Nian, Jing, Tao, Wang, Shuang, Su, and Zhuan—all famous, known as the "Eight Dragons." Their neighborhood was renamed Gaoyangli after the ancient Gaoyang family's eight talented members. Xun Jing, a virtuous man who refused office, died at fifty and was named Mr. Xuan Xing. Xun Shu's sons Xun Yu and Xun Tan both hated evil and sought to eradicate eunuchs, killing even minor offenders among eunuch branches. Xun Yu later died with General Dou Wu and Li Ying, while Xun Tan was imprisoned for life.
