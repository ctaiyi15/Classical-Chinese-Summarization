Yi yielded to Yu's son Qi, who became Emperor Qi of Xia after gaining universal support. Qi suppressed the Youhu clan's rebellion, issuing the "Gan's Oath" to enforce discipline. Subsequent Xia rulers included Taikang, who lost his country due to neglect; Zhongkang; and others down to Jie, the final emperor. Jie's tyranny led to his defeat by Tang, who replaced the Xia dynasty. Tang enfeoffed Xia descendants, who later received Qi in the Zhou dynasty. Yu's burial site at Kuaiji Mountain, meaning "accounting," commemorates his assessment of princes' merits.
