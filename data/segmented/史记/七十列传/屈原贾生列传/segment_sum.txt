summary line 3: After Qu Yuan's death, Chu scholars like Song Yu imitated his style but lacked his boldness. Chu weakened and was conquered by Qin decades later. Over a century later, Jia Yi of the Han Dynasty, while serving as Grand Tutor of Changsha, wrote a poem to honor Qu Yuan. Jia Yi, a talented young scholar from Luoyang, was favored by Emperor Wen of Han but slandered by officials like Zhou Bo, leading to his demotion. He wrote "Ode to the Birds" to console himself, reflecting on the cyclical nature of fortune and the futility of worldly concerns. Later, he served as tutor to King Liang Huai, who died in a riding accident. Jia Yi, blaming himself, died of grief at age 33. The historian Sima Qian, after reading Qu Yuan's works and Jia Yi's poems, initially criticized Qu Yuan for not seeking refuge elsewhere but later, after reading "Ode to the Birds," understood his equanimity toward life and death.
