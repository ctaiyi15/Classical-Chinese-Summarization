自古受命而王，王者之兴何尝不以卜筮决於天命哉！
其於周尤甚，及秦可见。
代王之入，任於卜者。
太卜之起，由汉兴而有。
司马季主者，楚人也。
卜於长安东市。
宋忠为中大夫，贾谊为博士，同日俱出洗沐，相从论议，诵易先王圣人之道术，究遍人情，相视而叹。
贾谊曰： 吾闻古之圣人，不居朝廷，必在卜医之中。
今吾已见三公九卿朝士大夫，皆可知矣。
试之卜数中以观采。
二人即同舆而之市，游於卜肆中。
天新雨，道少人，司马季主间坐，弟子三四人侍，方辩天地之道，日月之运，阴阳吉凶之本。
二大夫再拜谒。
司马季主视其状貌，如类有知者，即礼之，使弟子延之坐。
坐定，司马季主复理前语，分别天地之终始，日月星辰之纪，差次仁义之际，列吉凶之符，语数千言，莫不顺理。
宋忠、贾谊瞿然而悟，猎缨正襟危坐，曰： 吾望先生之状，听先生之辞，小子窃观於世，未尝见也。
translation: 
Since ancient times, only those who have received destiny can become kings, and the rise of kings does not depend on destiny through divination!
This situation was particularly prevalent in the Zhou Dynasty and can still be seen in the Qin Dynasty.
When the kings of the Han Dynasty came to Beijing to inherit the throne, they were also left to the fortune tellers.
As for the emergence of diviners, they began to appear since the rise of the Han Dynasty.
Lord Sima Ji is from Chu State.
He was doing divination in the east city of Chang'an.
At this time, Song Zhong was appointed as a doctor, and Jia Yi was appointed as a doctor. One day, the two of them went out to the court to take a bath and take a break. While walking, they discussed and learned the governance methods of the previous kings and saints, and extensively explored the world and human affairs. The two looked at each other and sighed.
Jia Yi said: I heard that ancient saints, if they were not officials in the court, must be among the ranks of diviners and doctors.
Now, I have seen the three princes, nine ministers and the sergeants and officials of the court, and I can know their talents, learning and character.
Let’s try to observe the soothsayer’s style.
The two of them went to the city in the same car and visited the divining restaurant.
It had just rained, and there were very few people on the road. Lord Sima Ji was sitting leisurely in the pavilion, with three or four disciples accompanying him, explaining the principles of heaven and earth, the movement of the sun and the moon, and the origin of good and bad luck in yin and yang.
The two doctors bowed twice to Lord Sima Ji.
Master Sima Ji looked at their appearance and saw that they seemed to be knowledgeable people, so he returned the courtesy and asked his disciples to lead them to sit down.
After sitting down, Sima Jizhu re-explained what he had said before, analyzed the origin and end of heaven and earth, the movement of the sun, moon and stars, distinguished the differences between benevolence and righteousness, listed the signs of good and bad luck, and talked about thousands of words, all of which were logical.
Song Zhong and Jia Yi were surprised and understood. They adjusted their crowns and belts, straightened their clothes, sat respectfully, and said, "I see your appearance and listen to your conversation. I have never seen anything like this in the world of today."

Kings historically received destiny without relying on divination, but by the Han dynasty, diviners had emerged. Lord Sima Ji, a diviner from Chu, practiced in Chang'an's east city. Two officials, Song Zhong and Jia Yi, visited him after discussing governance and saints. They questioned why Sima Ji, despite his wisdom, held a lowly position. Sima Ji defended divination, contrasting it with corrupt officials who exploit power for personal gain. He argued that diviners follow moral principles, aiding people through predictions and advice, unlike officials who engage in deceit and greed. He cited historical figures like Fuxi and King Wen of Zhou to highlight divination's value in governance. Sima Ji criticized the hypocrisy of those who judge diviners while ignoring their own ethical failings. He emphasized that diviners serve society by promoting virtue and preventing harm, often without recognition. The officials were humbled by his words. Later, Song Zhong and Jia Yi faced personal misfortunes—Song was convicted for a failed mission, and Jia Yi died after a student's accident. The text concludes with reflections on diviners' wisdom and their role in society, noting that many skilled individuals, like Sima Ji, remain unrecognized. The narrative underscores the tension between worldly status and moral integrity, advocating for humility and the pursuit of virtuous living.
