summary:
summary line 0: The Xiongnu, whose ancestor was Chunwei, a descendant of the Xia dynasty, were a nomadic people living in the northern wilds. They moved with their livestock—horses, cattle, sheep, camels, and donkeys—in search of water and pasture, without permanent cities or agriculture. They had no written language, relying on oral agreements. From childhood, they learned archery and riding, and all men were armored cavalry. Their customs prioritized warfare and plunder, favoring attack when advantageous and retreating without shame. They respected the strong and disdained the weak, practiced levirate marriage, and had no taboo against using personal names. Their leaders were called Chanyu, and they held seasonal gatherings for sacrifices and censuses. Laws were harsh, with punishments for murder and theft, and burial practices included human sacrifice for the Chanyu.

From the left and right virtuous kings down to the head of the household, those with high official positions have ten thousand cavalry, while those with small official positions have thousands of cavalry. There are a total of twenty-four officers, who are definitely named Wanqi.
The official positions of ministers are hereditary.
The Huyan family, the Lan family, and later the Xubu family, these three surnames were their nobles.
The kings and generals on the left will live in the east, as far as the east of Shanggu County, and the east is bordered by Uraccoon and Korea. The kings and generals on the right will live in the west, to the west of Shangjun, bordering the Yueshi, Shi, and Qiang. The location of Shanyu's royal court extended to Dai and Yunzhong counties. They each have their own land, and they move around in search of water and grass.
King Xian of the left and right and King Guli of the left and right are the largest. The princes of Gudu of the left and right assist Shan Yu in governing the country.
The twenty-four officers also set up subordinate officials such as Qianchang, Baichang, Shichang, Xiaowang, Xiang, Fengduwei, Duanhu, Qiequ and so on.
Every year in the first month of the year, all the officials have a small gathering in the court of Shan Yu to hold sacrifices.
In May, there is a big gathering in Mingcheng to worship ancestors, gods of heaven and earth, and ghosts and gods.
In autumn, when the horses are fat, there is a large gathering in the Pu Lin to assess and count the number of people and livestock.
