鲁仲连者，齐人也。
好奇伟俶傥之画策，而不肯仕宦任职，好持高节。
游於赵。
赵孝成王时，而秦王使白起破赵长平之军前後四十馀万，秦兵遂东围邯郸。
赵王恐，诸侯之救兵莫敢击秦军。
魏安釐王使将军晋鄙救赵，畏秦，止於荡阴不进。
魏王使客将军新垣衍间入邯郸，因平原君谓赵王曰： 秦所为急围赵者，前与齐湣王争彊为帝，已而复归帝；今齐已益弱，方今唯秦雄天下，此非必贪邯郸，其意欲复求为帝。
赵诚发使尊秦昭王为帝，秦必喜，罢兵去。
平原君犹预未有所决。
此时鲁仲连適游赵，会秦围赵，闻魏将欲令赵尊秦为帝，乃见平原君曰： 事将柰何？
平原君曰： 胜也何敢言事！
前亡四十万之众於外，今又内围邯郸而不能去。
魏王使客将军新垣衍令赵帝秦，今其人在是。
translation: 
Lu Zhonglian was a native of Qi.
He is good at analyzing unique, grand and extraordinary strategies, but he is unwilling to serve as an official and is willing to maintain high integrity.
He once visited Zhao State.
When Zhao Xiaocheng was king, King Zhao of Qin sent Bai Qi to lead an army to defeat Zhao Changping's army, killing more than 400,000 people in total. As a result, Qin's army advanced eastward and besieged Handan, the capital of Zhao.
King Zhao was very afraid, and no one from the reinforcements from the vassal states dared to attack the Qin army.
King Anli of Wei sent General Jin Bi to lead troops to rescue Zhao. However, he was afraid of the Qin army and stationed in Tangyin and did not dare to advance.
The king of Wei sent his guest general Xinyuan Yan into Handan through a hidden path, and met with the king of Zhao through Pingyuan Jun. He said: The reason why the Qin army is eager to besiege Zhao is because it used to compete with King Qi Min to become emperor, and soon King Qi Min revoked the title of emperor.
If Zhao can send envoys to honor King Qin Zhao as emperor, King Qin will be very happy and will withdraw his troops.
Mr. Pingyuan was hesitant and unable to make a decision.
At this time, Lu Zhonglian traveled to the State of Zhao and caught up with the Qin army to besiege Handan. He heard that the State of Wei wanted Zhao to honor King Qin Zhao and proclaim himself emperor, so he went to Jin to see Lord Pingyuan and said: "What are we going to do about this?"
Mr. Pingyuan said: How dare we talk about such a big event in our country!
Not long ago, 400,000 troops were lost abroad, and now they are surrounded by the Qin army in Handan, and they cannot make them retreat.
The King of Wei sent the guest general Xinyuan Yan to ask the State of Zhao to honor King Qin Zhao as emperor. This man is still here.

summary:
summary line 0: Lu Zhonglian, a native of Qi, was skilled in grand strategies but refused to serve as an official, valuing integrity. During the siege of Handan by Qin, Zhao sought aid. Wei sent General Jin Bi, who hesitated, and envoy Xinyuan Yan proposed Zhao honor King Zhao of Qin as emperor to secure peace. Pingyuan Jun hesitated. Lu Zhonglian confronted Xinyuan Yan, arguing that submitting to Qin would bring disaster. He cited historical examples, such as King Wei of Qi’s humiliation by Zhou and the fates of Jiuhou and Ehou under Yin Zhou, to illustrate the dangers of imperial ambition. Lu declared he would rather die than serve Qin. Xinyuan Yan was convinced and withdrew his proposal. The Qin army retreated after Wei’s Prince Wuji rescued Zhao. Pingyuan Jun offered Lu a reward, but he refused, stating that a worthy person resolves conflicts without seeking payment, and left forever.

孝王怒，下之吏，将欲杀之。
邹阳客游，以谗见禽，恐死而负累，乃从狱中上书曰：臣闻忠无不报，信不见疑，臣常以为然，徒虚语耳。
昔者荆轲慕燕丹之义，白虹贯日，太子畏之；卫先生为秦画长平之事，太白蚀昴，而昭王疑之。
夫精变天地而信不喻两主，岂不哀哉！
今臣尽忠竭诚，毕议原知，左右不明，卒从吏讯，为世所疑，是使荆轲、卫先生复起，而燕、秦不悟也。
translation: 
I hope you will choose one of these options and take action!
General Yan read Lu Zhonglian's letter and cried for several days, but still hesitated to make a decision.
He wanted to return to the Yan State, but there was already a rift, and he was afraid of being killed; he wanted to surrender to the Qi State, but too many Qi people had been killed and captured, and he was afraid that he would be humiliated after surrendering.
He sighed deeply and said: It is better to commit suicide than to let others kill me.
Just committed suicide.
Liaocheng was in chaos, so Tian Dan marched into Liaocheng and bloodbathed Liaocheng.
When he returned, he reported Lu Zhonglian's affairs to the King of Qi. The King of Qi wanted to knight him.
After hearing this, Lu Zhonglian fled to the seaside and lived in seclusion. He said: Instead of being rich and serving others, it is better for me to be poor and despise the world and let my own ambitions go.
Zou Yang is from Qi State.
When he traveled to Liang State, he interacted with Zhuang Ji, a native of Wu State, and Mei Cheng, a native of Huaiyin.
He wrote a letter of self-recommendation and was a disciple of King Liang Xiao among Yang Sheng and Gongsun Gui.
Yang Sheng and others were jealous of Zou Yang and spoke ill of him in front of King Xiao of Liang.
King Xiao of Liang was very angry. He handed Zou Yang over to his subordinate officials and wanted to kill him.
Zou Yang traveled in the Liang Kingdom and was arrested for being slandered. Worried about being accused of unwarranted charges after his death, he wrote a letter to King Xiao of Liang from prison. He wrote: I heard that loyal people will be rewarded and honest people will not be suspected. I always thought it was right in the past, but today it seems to be just an empty talk.
In the past, Gao Yi, whom Jing Ke admired from Prince Dan of Yan, went to assassinate the King of Qin. Although there was a sign of a white rainbow penetrating the sun in the sky, Prince Dan of Yan was still worried that Jing Ke would regret it and did not dare to go; Mr. Wei planned the Changping event for the King of Qin, and there was also a sign that Venus would cover the Pleiades, but King Zhao of Qin was still full of doubts.
Their sincerity moved the world and showed signs, but they were not understood by the two masters Yan Dan and Zhao Wang. Isn't this sad!
Now I am trying my best to be loyal and give my best advice, hoping that your Majesty will adopt it. The people around you did not understand the situation and finally handed me over to the officials for interrogation. I was misunderstood by the world. Even if Jing Ke and Wei were resurrected, King Yan Dan and Qin Zhao would not wake up.

summary:
summary line 1: Over twenty years later, a Yan general held Qi’s Liaocheng. Lu Zhonglian wrote him a letter, urging him to choose wisely: return to Yan or surrender to Qi. He argued that clinging to a besieged city was unwise, citing Guan Zhong and Cao Mo as examples of those who overcame disgrace to achieve greatness. The general, torn between fear of execution and shame, committed suicide, leading to Qi’s capture of Liaocheng. Qi’s king offered Lu a title, but he fled to the seaside, preferring poverty to serving others.
