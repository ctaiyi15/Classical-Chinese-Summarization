荆王刘贾，高帝从父兄也，不知其初起时。
汉元年，还定三秦，贾为将军，定塞地，从东击项籍。
汉王败成皋，北度河，得张耳、韩信军，军修武，深沟高垒，使贾将二万人，骑数百，击楚，度白马津入楚地，烧其积聚，以破其业，无以给项王军食。
已而楚兵击之，贾辄避不肯与战，而与彭越相保。
汉王追项籍至固陵，使贾南度淮围寿春。
还至，使人间招楚大司马周殷。
周殷反楚，佐贾举九江，迎英布兵，皆会垓下，诛项籍。
汉王因使贾将九江兵，与太尉卢绾西南击临江王共尉，尉死，以临江为南郡。
贾既有功，而高祖子弱，昆弟少，又不贤，欲王同姓以填天下，乃下诏曰： 将军刘贾有功，及择子弟可以为王者。
群臣皆曰： 立刘贾为荆王，王淮东。
立六年，而淮南王黥布反，东击荆。
贾与战，弗胜，走富陵，为布军所杀。
燕王刘泽，高祖从祖昆弟也。
高祖三年，泽为郎中。
十一年，以将军击陈豨将王黄，封为营陵侯。
高后时，齐人田生游乏资，以画奸泽。
泽大说之，用金二百斤为田生寿。田生已得金，即归齐。
二岁，泽使人谓田生曰： 弗与矣。
田生如长安，不见泽，而假大宅，令其子求事吕后所幸大谒者张卿。
居数月，田生子请张卿临，亲修具。
张卿往，见田生帷帐具置如列侯。张卿惊。

Jing Wang Liu Jia, the cousin of Mo Gaozu Liu Bang, did not know when he participated in the uprising.
In the first year of Han Dynasty, when the king of Han Dynasty returned to the Three Qin Dynasty, Liu Mai was appointed as the general. After pacifying Sima Xin's territory, he followed Liu Bangdong to attack Xiangji.
The King of Han was defeated by Chenggao, crossed the Yellow River to the north, captured the armies of Zhang Er and Han Xin, and stationed their troops in Xiuwu County. On the one hand, they dug deep trenches and built high barriers; on the other hand, they sent Liu Mai to lead 20,000 troops and hundreds of cavalry to attack Chu, crossed Baima Jin, and entered the Chu land. They burned Chu's grain and grass, destroyed the Chu army's military supply, and made it impossible to supply food to King Xiang's army.
Soon, the Chu soldiers attacked, but Liu Jia always avoided it and did not fight the Chu soldiers. Instead, he and Peng Yue relied on each other to protect themselves.
The king of Han pursued Xiang Ji to Guling and sent Liu Mai to lead his troops south across the Huaihe River to surround Shouchun.
After returning, he sent people to alienate and recruit Zhou Yin, the great commander of Chu State, to surrender.
Zhou Yin rebelled against Chu and returned to Han, assisted Liu Mai in capturing Jiujiang, catered to Yingbu's army, fought at Gaixia, and defeated Xiang Yu.
Therefore, the King of Han sent Liu Jia to lead the Jiujiang soldiers, together with Taiwei Lu Wan, to attack Linjiang King Gongwei in the southwest. After Wei Gong's death, Linjiang was renamed Nanjun.
Since Liu Jia had achieved great military exploits, the emperor's son at that time was young, had few brothers, and was not very talented and virtuous. He wanted to make someone with the same surname king to conquer the world, so Xia Shao said: "General Liu Mai has meritorious service, and he is qualified to choose the king among his descendants."
The ministers said: Let Liu Mai be made King of Jing and take charge of Huaidong.
Six years later, King Yingbu of Huainan rebelled and attacked Jingdi eastward.
Liu Mai fought with him but failed to win. He fled to Fuling County and was killed by Yingbu's pursuers.
Liu Ze, King of Yan, was the brother of Gaozu and his great-grandfather.
In the third year of Emperor Gaozu's reign, Liu Ze became a doctor.
In the eleventh year of Emperor Gaozu's reign, as a general, he led troops to defeat Chen Xi's general Wang Huang and was named Yinglinghou.
When Empress Gao was in power, Tian Sheng, a native of Qi, was short of funds when lobbying outside, so he turned to Liu Ze for help by offering suggestions.
Liu Ze was very happy and gave Tian Sheng two hundred pounds of gold when he passed away. Tian Sheng got the gold and immediately returned to Qi.
The following year, Liu Ze sent a messenger to Tian Sheng: We are no longer friends!
When Tian Sheng came to Chang'an, he did not go to see Liu Ze. Instead, he stayed in a big house and asked his son to find a way to work under Zhang Qing, the great emissary favored by Empress Lu.
A few months later, Tian Sheng's son invited Zhang Qing to his home as a guest, and Tian Sheng hosted a banquet for him in person.
Zhang Qing went there and was greatly surprised when he saw that the curtains and other decorative appliances wrapped in Tiansheng's house were in compliance with the standards of the princes.

summary:
summary line 0: The histories of three Han dynasty princes: Liu Jia (King of Jing), Liu Ze (King of Yan), and Liu Bi (King of Wu). Liu Jia, a cousin of Emperor Gaozu, served as a general, leading campaigns against Chu forces, burning supplies, and later capturing Jiujiang. He was made King of Jing but was killed in battle against the rebel Yingbu. Liu Ze, a brother of Gaozu, was initially a marquis. Through a scheme by Tian Sheng, he was made King of Langye after persuading Empress Lu to enfeoff the Lü clan. After Empress Lu’s death, he joined others in supporting Emperor Xiaowen and was renamed King of Yan. His descendants included a son, Kang Wang, and a grandson, Dingguo, who committed adultery with his father’s concubine and sisters, leading to his suicide and the abolition of his state. Liu Bi, son of Liu Zhong, was made King of Wu after defeating Yingbu. He grew wealthy from copper and salt, and after his son was killed by the crown prince, he feigned illness and plotted rebellion. During Emperor Jing’s reign, Chao Cuo advocated reducing the princes’ territories, prompting Liu Bi to lead a rebellion of seven states (Wu, Chu, Zhao, Jiaoxi, Jiaodong, Jinan, and Zichuan) in 154 BCE. Liu Bi issued a call to arms, offering rewards for killing Han officials. Emperor Jing executed Chao Cuo to appease the rebels, but the rebellion continued. Zhou Yafu, a Han general, cut off Wu’s supply lines and defeated them. Liu Bi fled to Dongyue, where he was killed. The other rebel princes were also defeated or committed suicide. The text concludes that Liu Jia’s kingship was due to the unstable times, Liu Ze’s was based on a risky scheme, and Liu Bi’s rebellion stemmed from his wealth and personal grievance, leading to disaster.
