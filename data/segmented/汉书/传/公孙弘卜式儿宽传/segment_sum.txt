summary:
summary line 0: Gongsun Hong, born in Xue County, Sichuan State, began his career as a jailer but was dismissed for a crime. Left impoverished, he herded pigs until his forties, when he started studying the "Spring and Autumn" and various philosophical texts. At sixty, he was recruited as a virtuous and literary person under Emperor Wu of Han, but his mission to the Huns displeased the emperor, leading to his dismissal. In the fifth year of Yuanguang, he was recommended again and, despite initial reluctance, presented a detailed policy response. His essay emphasized governance through righteousness, trust, and eight principles: appointing by talent, avoiding useless discussions, reducing taxes, not harming agriculture, promoting the virtuous, rewarding merit, punishing crime appropriately, and rewarding virtue. He argued that harmony between heaven and earth arises from unified virtue, and that floods and droughts under Yu and Tang were due to prior misrule. His response was ranked first by the emperor, who appointed him as a doctor.

He served as imperial censor and later prime minister, receiving the title Marquis of Pingjin. During the Huainan and Hengshan rebellions, he fell ill and offered to resign, but the emperor refused, granting him leave. He died in office at eighty.
