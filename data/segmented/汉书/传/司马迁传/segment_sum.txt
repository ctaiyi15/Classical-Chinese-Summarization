summary:
summary line 1: Sima Tan studied astronomy, the Book of Changes, and Taoism, and served under Emperor Wu of Han. He worried that scholars misunderstood the six schools of thought—Yin-Yang, Confucianism, Mohism, Legalism, Mingjia, and Taoism—and discussed their principles. He noted that Yin-Yang had many taboos but its seasonal order was useful; Confucianism was broad but lacked essentials, though its rituals were important; Mohism was frugal but hard to follow, yet its emphasis on economy was valuable; Legalism was harsh but upheld the ruler-minister distinction; Mingjia was too formal but its name-reality dialectic was worth considering; and Taoism united spirit and action, absorbed strengths of other schools, and was concise and effective. He criticized Confucianism for tiring the ruler and praised Taoism for its simplicity and adaptability.
