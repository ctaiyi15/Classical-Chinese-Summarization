The huge profits from the last industry cannot be obtained in vain, so officials are not afraid of breaking the law and extorting money, and warriors with swords compete to kill people, cheat and plunder, but the people of the world do not know shame, so treacherous and evil behaviors gradually grow.
Beautiful and rare things are originally beautiful to look at and pleasant to listen to, so if you lose your cultivation, you will become arrogant; if you don’t have healthy music, you will become immoral; if you lose etiquette and justice, you will be literal; if you give up education, you will become hypocritical.
Treachery, deceit, excess, lewdness, and arrogance—these are all behaviors that cannot be used to legislate and educate the people.

summary:
summary line 0: Yan An, from Linmiao, wrote a letter as the late Prime Minister Shi, criticizing the extravagance of the wealthy, who decorated their carriages, clothes, and palaces excessively, leading to social ills like greed, lawlessness, and moral decay. He argued that luxury without restraint causes dissatisfaction, drives people from their proper occupations, and fosters treachery and violence. Yan An proposed laws to curb extravagance, reduce envy, and promote harmony, which would lead to peace, reduced crime, and natural prosperity. He contrasted the Zhou Dynasty's long rule with its decline due to weak leadership, leading to the Warring States period and the rise of the Five Hegemons, who upheld the emperor's authority. The Qin Dynasty, under Emperor Qin Shi Huang, unified China but failed to reform its harsh laws and heavy taxes, instead expanding aggressively against the Xiongnu and Baiyue, causing widespread suffering and rebellion. Yan An warned the Han court against similar overreach, advocating for domestic stability over foreign conquests, and noted that the Zhou's weakness and Qin's strength both led to their downfalls due to inflexibility.

translation: 
Jia Ganzhi, courtesy name Junfang, is the great-grandson of Jia Yi.
When Emperor Yuan first came to the throne, he wrote a book about the gains and losses of political affairs, and was summoned to wait for the imperial edict at Jinmamen.
At the beginning, Emperor Wu conquered South Vietnam and established Daner County and Zhukuang County in the first year of Yuanfeng. These two counties were both located on the continent in the southern sea. They were about a thousand miles wide, with a total of 16 counties and more than 23,000 households.
The people in Nahuo were irritable and love affairs. They thought they were separated from the Han Dynasty and violated the officials' prohibitions many times. The officials also treated them cruelly. They rebelled once every few years and killed the officials. The Han government sent troops to attack and quell their rebellion.
From the establishment of the county to the first year of Emperor Zhao's reign, there were a total of six rebellions in more than 20 years.
In the fifth year of the First Yuan Dynasty, Dan'er County was abandoned and belonged to Zhukuang County.
In the third year of Emperor Xuan's reign, three counties in Zhukuang County rebelled again.
Seven years after this rebellion, in the first year of Ganlu, nine more counties rebelled. The Han government sent troops to quell these two rebellions.
In the first year of Emperor Yuan's reign, there was another rebellion in Zhuli County, and the Han court sent troops to attack.
Counties are even more rebelling against each other, and the situation is uncertain year after year.
Emperor Yuan discussed with relevant ministers about dispatching large forces to suppress the attack, but Jia Xian suggested that they should not attack.
The emperor sent his consort Prince Consort Lechanghou Wang Shang to interrogate Jia Gianzhi, saying: Zhuli Nei has been a county for a long time. Now it has betrayed and disobeyed the imperial order, but you say that it should not be attacked and encourage the rebellion of the barbarians. It is a disservice to the late emperor. In the Six Classics, which section should it belong to?

summary:
summary line 3: Jia Ganzhi, great-grandson of Jia Yi, advised Emperor Yuan against attacking Zhuli County (in modern Hainan), which had repeatedly rebelled since its establishment. He argued that the territory was not worth the cost, citing historical precedents like Yao, Shun, and the Zhou Dynasty, which did not forcibly conquer distant lands. He contrasted Emperor Wen's frugality and peace with Emperor Wu's costly expansions, which led to widespread suffering and rebellion. Emperor Yuan accepted his advice and abandoned Zhuli County. Later, Jia Ganzhi and Yang Xing, a favored official, conspired to recommend each other for high positions, but Shi Xian reported their scheme. They were tried for deceit and leaking palace secrets; Jia Ganzhi was executed, and Yang Xing was reduced to penal servitude.
