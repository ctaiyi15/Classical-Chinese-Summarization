Despite Pei Gong's disdain for Confucian scholars, Li Shiqi boldly criticized him for washing his feet while receiving visitors. He then volunteered to persuade the King of Qi to submit to Han, successfully convincing Tian Guang to surrender. However, Han Xin attacked Qi anyway, and Tian Guang, believing Li Shiqi had deceived him, executed him.

summary:
summary line 1: Lu Jia of Chu was a skilled orator who served as an envoy for Emperor Gaozu. He was sent to South Vietnam to grant Wei Tuo the king's seal. When Wei Tuo received him disrespectfully, Lu Jia warned him that the Han emperor could easily destroy him. Wei Tuo apologized and acknowledged Han's superiority. Lu Jia successfully secured South Vietnam's submission. Later, when Emperor Gaozu dismissed the value of studying the classics, Lu Jia argued that while one could conquer the world on horseback, one could not govern it that way. He wrote twelve essays explaining why Qin fell and Han succeeded, which became known as the "New Speeches." During Empress Dowager Lu's regency, Lu Jia retired but later advised Chancellor Chen Ping to ally with General Zhou Bo to counter the Lu family's conspiracy. His strategy helped preserve the Liu imperial house.

summary:
summary line 4: Shusun Tong of Xue served under Qin II, cleverly avoiding punishment by calling rebels mere thieves. He later served Xiang Liang, King Huai, and finally surrendered to the King of Han. Noticing the disorderly behavior of ministers at court, he offered to create court rituals. Despite criticism from conservative Confucian scholars, he adapted ancient and Qin rituals to create a practical system. After the completion of Changle Palace, the new rituals were implemented, impressing Emperor Gaozu, who remarked, "Now I know the dignity of being emperor." Shusun Tong was appointed Fengchang and rewarded with gold, which he distributed to his disciples. He later served as Crown Prince Tutor and successfully dissuaded Emperor Gaozu from replacing the crown prince. Under Emperor Xiaohui, he continued to formulate rituals and established the practice of offering fruits at ancestral temples.
