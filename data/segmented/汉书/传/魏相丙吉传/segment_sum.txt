愿少慎事自重，臧器于身。
相心善其言，为霁威严。
居部二岁，征为谏大夫，复为河南太守。
数年，宣帝即位，征相入为大司农，迁御史大夫。
translation: 
When Che Qianqiu, the son of an arsenal commander, arrived in Chang'an, General Huo Guang scolded Prime Minister Wei for this: The young new emperor had just ascended the throne and believed that Hangu Pass was the strongest place to defend the capital and the weapons depot was a place where sophisticated weapons were stored, so he made the prime minister's brother the captain of Hangu Pass and the prime minister's son the commander of the weapons depot.
Now the governor of Henan, Wei Xiang, did not think deeply about the country's plans, but just dismissed the prime minister's son when he saw his death. How shallow is this!
Later, someone accused Prime Minister Wei of killing an innocent person, and the matter was brought to the competent government office.
There were two to three thousand officers from the Henan garrison who stopped the general Huo Guang and said they were willing to stay for another year to atone for the sin of the prefect Wei Xiang.
More than 10,000 old and weak people in Henan guarded Hangu Pass and asked to enter the pass to write to the emperor. The officials guarding the pass reported the matter.
General Huo Guang handed Wei Prime Minister to Tingwei for punishment because of the arsenal order.
Prime Minister Wei was imprisoned for a long time. After the winter, it happened that the imperial court pardoned the prisoners.
There was also an edict ordering Prime Minister Wei to serve as the magistrate of Maoling County again and promote him to the governor of Yangzhou.
Later, the imperial court inspected the ministers of the county and state, and many of them were demoted.
Prime Minister Wei and Bing Ji were close. At that time, Bing Ji's official position was Doctor Guanglu. He wrote to Prime Minister Wei and said: The imperial court has a deep understanding of your achievements and behavior and will appoint you.
I hope you will be cautious and self-respecting in everything you do and cultivate your talents.
Prime Minister Wei thought Bingji's words were right, so he restrained his dignity.
After serving as governor for two years, he was recruited as an admonishing official and then became the governor of Henan.
A few years later, Emperor Xuan ascended the throne and recruited Wei Xiang to join the imperial court as chief minister of agriculture, and was later promoted to imperial censor.

summary:
summary line 0: Wei Xiang, courtesy name Wenweng, was a native of Dingtao, Jiyin, later moving to Pingling. He studied the Book of Changes in his youth, became a county governor, and was praised for his virtue. After ranking first in policy questions, he became magistrate of Maoling County. When a guest of Censor Sang Hongyang falsely claimed Sang was arriving and tied up the magistrate, Wei Xiang suspected foul play, arrested the guest, tortured him to reveal his crime, and executed him in the street, leading to excellent governance in Maoling. Promoted to governor of Henan, he prohibited evil deeds, instilling fear in local powers. When Prime Minister Che Qianqiu died, his son, commander of the Luoyang Armory, resigned fearing Wei Xiang’s strictness. Wei Xiang regretted this, fearing it would cause resentment. General Huo Guang later scolded Wei Xiang for dismissing the prime minister’s son after his death, calling it shallow. Accused of killing an innocent person, Wei Xiang faced investigation, but over 10,000 Henan residents petitioned for him. Huo Guang handed him to Tingwei for punishment, but he was pardoned and reappointed as magistrate of Maoling, then governor of Yangzhou. After demotions in an imperial inspection, Wei Xiang, close to Bingji, heeded his advice to restrain his dignity. He served as governor for two years, then became an admonishing official and later governor of Henan again. After Emperor Xuan ascended, Wei Xiang became chief minister of agriculture, then imperial censor. Four years later, Huo Guang died. Wei Xiang wrote to the emperor, warning against the Huo family’s power, citing historical precedents of autocratic rule causing chaos. He suggested weakening their influence to strengthen the Han dynasty. Emperor Xuan adopted his advice, deposing Huo Yu, Huo Yun, and Huo Shan. Wei Xiang became prime minister, replacing Wei Xian, and was named Gaopinghou. The Huo family later plotted to kill him but were discovered and executed. Emperor Xuan then personally governed, selecting good ministers, and Wei Xiang was popular as prime minister. During the Yuankang period, when the Xiongnu attacked Han forces, Wei Xiang advised against military retaliation, citing border poverty and internal social issues like 222 cases of familial killings. He urged focus on domestic stability. Emperor Xuan followed his advice. Wei Xiang, well-versed in the Book of Changes, studied Han history and advocated following past systems. He presented 23 items from the late emperor’s edicts, emphasizing agriculture, grain storage, and frugality to prepare for disasters. He warned of current grain price hikes and border conflicts, urging the emperor to care for the people. Emperor Xuan implemented his suggestions. Wei Xiang also presented chapters on Yin-Yang and seasonal governance, arguing that proper seasonal management prevents disasters and ensures prosperity. He recommended selecting ministers knowledgeable in Yin-Yang to oversee seasonal affairs. The emperor adopted his proposals. Wei Xiang’s commissioners reported irregularities from counties, often revealing unreported rebellions or disasters. He was severe and resolute, unlike the tolerant Bingji. He served as prime minister for nine years, dying in the third year of the Shenjue era, and was posthumously named Xianhou. His son Wei Hong inherited the title but was demoted to Marquis of Guannei during the Ganlu era due to a crime.
