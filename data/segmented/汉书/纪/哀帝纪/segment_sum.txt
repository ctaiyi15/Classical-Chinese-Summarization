original: 
孝哀皇帝，元帝庶孙，定陶恭王子也。
母曰丁姬。
年三岁嗣立为王，长好文辞法律。
元延四年入朝，尽从傅、相、中尉。
时成帝少弟中山孝王亦来朝，独从傅。
上怪之，以问定陶王，对曰： 令，诸侯王朝，得从其国二千石。
傅、相、中尉皆国二千石，故尽从之。
上令诵《诗》，通习，能说。
他日问中山王： 独从傅在何法令？
不能对。
令诵《尚书》，又废。
及赐食于前，后饱；起下，袜系解。
成帝由此以为不能，而贤定陶王，数称其材。
时王祖母傅太后随王来朝，私赂遗上所幸赵昭仪及帝舅票骑将军曲阳侯王根。
昭仪及根见上亡子，亦欲豫自结为长久计，皆更称定陶王，劝帝以为嗣。
成帝亦自美其材，为加元服而遣之，时年十七矣。
明年，使执金吾任宏守大鸿胪，持节征定陶王，立为皇太子。
谢曰： 臣幸得继父守籓为诸侯王，材质不足以假充太子之宫。
translation: 
Emperor Xiaoai was the grandson of Emperor Yuan of the Han Dynasty and the son of Prince Dingtao Gong.
The mother is Ding Ji.
He became king at the age of three, and became interested in literature and law as an adult.
In the fourth year of Yuanyan's reign, he went to the capital to see Emperor Cheng, and his Fu, Xiang, and lieutenants all came together.
At this time, Emperor Cheng's younger brother, Prince Xiao of Zhongshan, also came to see him, but only Fu Tong came.
The emperor was puzzled, so he asked King Dingtao. King Dingtao replied: "The imperial court has issued an order. When princes and kings come to court, their officials with a title of two thousand stones should come together."
Fu, Xiang, and Lieutenant all have two thousand stones, so they should all come together.
The emperor asked him to recite "Poetry", and he was familiar with it and could explain its meaning.
Another day, Emperor Cheng asked King Zhongshan: "Which decree is it based on when Fu Tong comes?"
King Zhongshan was speechless.
I asked him to recite the Book of Documents, but he forgot about it again.
Emperor Cheng invited him to dinner, but he was still eating after everyone else had finished. When he left the table, even his garter belt fell off.
When Emperor Cheng saw this, he thought King Zhongshan was incompetent, but he liked King Dingtao very much and often praised his talents.
At this time, Queen Mother Fu, the grandmother of King Dingtao, accompanied her to the court and privately presented gifts to Emperor Cheng's favorite Zhao Zhaoyi and his uncle, Hussar General and Quyanghou Wanggen.
Zhao Zhaoyi and Wang Gen saw that the emperor had no children and wanted to establish a relationship with a suitable prince as soon as possible to maintain their long-term wealth, so they praised King Dingtao and persuaded Emperor Cheng to make him the prince.
Emperor Cheng also admired King Dingtao's talents, so he held an adult crowning ceremony for him in the court and sent him back to his fiefdom, Dingtao. He was seventeen years old at this time.
In the second year, the emperor sent Jinwu Renhong to act as Dahonglu. With the imperial edict, he summoned King Dingtao and established him as the crown prince.
King Dingtao thanked Zhao and said: I am fortunate enough to inherit my late father's vassal status and become the prince of the vassals. However, my talents and virtues are not enough to enter the prince's palace.

summary:
summary line 0: Emperor Xiaoai, grandson of Emperor Yuan and son of Prince Dingtao Gong, became king at age three and developed an interest in literature and law. In the fourth year of Yuanyan's reign, he visited Emperor Cheng with his officials, while Prince Xiao of Zhongshan came with only one attendant. Emperor Cheng, impressed by King Dingtao's knowledge of the "Poetry" and his explanations, favored him over the incompetent King Zhongshan. Queen Mother Fu, King Dingtao's grandmother, bribed Emperor Cheng's favorites Zhao Zhaoyi and Wang Gen, who, seeing the emperor had no heirs, praised King Dingtao. Emperor Cheng crowned him at seventeen and later made him crown prince. King Dingtao humbly declined but accepted. After Emperor Cheng's death in the second year of Suihe, the prince became emperor, honoring the Queen Mother and issuing amnesties. He honored his grandmother and mother, granted titles to relatives, and dismissed Yuefu for lewd music. He rewarded Wang Gen and others for supporting his succession and granted lands to Wang Liang for mourning. He issued edicts to curb extravagance, limiting land and slave ownership, banning merchants from land and office, and stopping complex embroidery. He revoked certain laws, allowed inner palace residents under thirty to marry, freed slaves over fifty, and banned rare animal tributes. He increased salaries for low-ranking officials and punished corrupt officials. After disasters like earthquakes and floods, he sent officials to aid victims and exempted taxes for the poor. In the first year of Jianping, he pardoned the world, deposed guilty marquises, and gave Wang family lands to the poor. He sought talent, granted money and cloth to officials, and saw the suicide of Empress Dowager Feng and her brother. In the second year, he dismissed the chief minister, restored the imperial censor, and honored his ancestors with new titles and temples. After Empress Dowager Ding's death, he followed Zhou rites for burial, recruiting 50,000 workers. He initially accepted prophecies to change the era name but later revoked them, punishing the prophets and officials. He faced droughts, fires, and comets, restored sacrifices, and dealt with rebellions. He granted titles for reporting crimes, honored his wife, and sought military talent. In the first year of Yuanshou, a solar eclipse led to an edict blaming himself and seeking virtuous officials. After Empress Dowager Fu's death, he dismissed officials and saw portents. In the second year, Xiongnu envoys visited, and he divided official duties. Emperor Ai died in Weiyang Palace at age 20 after six years as emperor, buried in Yiling. Ban Gu noted his early talent and efforts to strengthen authority but lamented his short reign.
