summary:
summary line 2: The text discusses the evolution of criminal law from ancient times. The Zhou Dynasty established three categories of laws—light, medium, and heavy—with specific punishments for different crimes. After the Zhou decline, King Mu expanded the penal code to 3,000 items. During the Spring and Autumn period, Zichan of Zheng cast criminal laws on a tripod, drawing criticism from Jin's Shuxiang, who argued that public knowledge of laws would lead to disputes and decline. Confucius emphasized moral guidance over legal coercion. The Warring States period saw harsh laws under Shen Buhai and Shang Yang, culminating in Qin Shihuang's oppressive regime, which led to widespread rebellion. The Han Dynasty initially simplified laws under Gaozu, but later emperors like Xiaowen and Xiaojing reformed punishments, abolishing corporal punishment and reducing flogging. Despite these efforts, legal complexity grew, and by Emperor Wu's reign, laws became increasingly strict, with over 13,000 capital offenses. Emperor Xuan and Emperor Yuan attempted to simplify laws, but reforms were incomplete.
