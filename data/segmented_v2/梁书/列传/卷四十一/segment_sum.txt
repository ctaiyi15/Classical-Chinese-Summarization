Chu Xiang, courtesy name Shiju, lost his parents young and became knowledgeable. He served as Secretary Lang and various posts, known for his uprightness and handsome appearance. He died in office at age forty-four, and his cousin Xie Gao wrote an epitaph praising his character. Chu Xiang was filial and once saw a strange light while praying for his mother's illness, which was cured. Xiao Jie, courtesy name Mao Jing, was knowledgeable and good at writing. He served as Prince Sheren and Shangshu Jinbulang, with outstanding political achievements. He was appointed as prefect of Shixing and later as Shaofu Qing. He advised against accepting Hou Jing, warning of his treacherous nature, but Emperor Gaozu did not adopt his advice. Xiao Jie died at seventy-three. Xiao Qia, courtesy name Hongcheng, was smart and loved learning. He served as prefect of Southern Xuzhou, known for his honesty. He died at fifty-five, leaving twenty volumes of collected works. Chu Qiu, courtesy name Zhongbao, was poor but studious. He served as magistrate of Liyang and later as censor Zhongcheng, known for his fairness. He died at seventy. Liu Ru, courtesy name Xiaozhi, could write at seven and was filial. He served as Tai Mo Ling and was admired by Emperor Gaozu. He died at fifty-nine, leaving twenty volumes of collected works. His brothers Liu Lan and Liu Zun were also notable. Liu Qian, courtesy name Xiaoyi, was a generous person who served his widowed sister-in-law respectfully. He died at sixty-seven, leaving twenty volumes of collected works. His brothers Liu Xiaosheng and Liu Xiaowei were also talented. Yin Yun, courtesy name Guanshu, was free-spirited and studious, dying at fifty-nine. Xiao Ji, courtesy name Dexuan, could write at ten and was gentle and self-reliant. He died in office as prefect of Xin'an. The historian comments that these individuals were all famous and showed their talents when opportunities arose.
