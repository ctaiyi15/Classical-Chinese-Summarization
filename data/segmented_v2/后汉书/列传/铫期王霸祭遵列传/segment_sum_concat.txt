If he stands still and procrastinates for a while now, it will prompt him to deepen his deception and make Gongsun Shu increase his security, so it is better to march directly. Guangwu obeyed and sent Ji Zun as the vanguard.
Kaixiao sent his general Wang Yuan to stay at Longdi, and Ji Zun attacked, defeated Wang Yuan's army, and pursued him to Xinguan.
When the generals arrived, they fought with Kai Xiao but failed, so they retreated to Long.
Guangwu ordered Ji Zun to garrison troops in Qi, ordered Geng Yan to garrison troops in Qi, ordered General Feng Yi to garrison troops in the city, and ordered Grand Sima Wu Han and others to return to Chang'an.
Since then, Ji Zun has defeated Kuai Xiao several times.

In the autumn of the eighth year of Jianwu, he went up to Long from Guangwu.
When Kuai Xiao was destroyed, Guang Wu returned eastward and passed through Qian. He went to the Ji Zun military camp, comforted and fed the soldiers, and performed Huangmen martial arts, which stopped late at night.
At this time, Ji Zun was ill and was given a thick mattress to sit on, which was covered with the emperor's royal canopy.
Then let him march to Longxia.
When Gongsun Shu sent troops to rescue Wei Xiao, Wu Han, Geng Yan and others all fled back, except for Ji Zun who remained.
In the spring of the ninth year of Jianwu, he died in the army.
Ji Zun was an honest, conscientious, careful, self-denial, and dedicated to the public service. He always gave all the rewards he received to his soldiers. He had no private property at home. He wore a Wei belt, a quilt, and a quilt, and his wife's clothes were not colorful. Therefore, the emperor valued him.
After his death, sympathy and mourning were especially intense.
When the coffin arrived in Henan County, the emperor sent hundreds of officials to gather at the funeral site. Guangwu came in white mourning clothes and looked at the coffin in mourning.
summary:
**Ji Zun** (younger brother's grandson) was from Yingyang, Yingchuan. Though wealthy, he was respectful and frugal. After killing a bullying government official, he joined Guangwu. As a military commander, he executed Guangwu's own son for breaking the law, earning Guangwu's respect. He became a partial general and Marquis of Yingyang. Ji Zun fought bravely despite arrow wounds, defeated Zhang Man by cutting his food supply, and captured him alive. He attacked Zhang Feng, whose rebellion stemmed from a Taoist priest's deception about a jade seal. Ji Zun resisted Peng Chong for over a year, and after Peng's death, pacified his territory. In the sixth year of Jianwu, he argued for direct attack against Kui Xiao, serving as vanguard. He remained steadfast when others retreated, dying in the army in the ninth year of Jianwu. Ji Zun was known for self-denial, giving rewards to soldiers, owning no private property, and wearing plain clothes. Guangwu mourned deeply, personally attending his funeral. Posthumously named Marquis Cheng, Ji Zun was praised for his purity, loyalty, and Confucian conduct—selecting talents by Confucian standards, reciting poetry, and establishing a Confucian temple. He had no children, so his title was abolished.
