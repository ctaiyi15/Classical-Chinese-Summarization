乌桓鲜卑
乌桓者，本东胡也。汉初，匈奴冒顿灭其国，余类保乌桓山，因以为号焉。
俗善骑射，弋猎禽兽为事。
随水草放牧，居无常处。
以穹庐为舍，东开向日。
食肉饮酪，以毛毳为衣。
贵少而贱老，其性悍塞。
怒则杀父兄，而终不害其母，以母有族类，父兄无相仇报敌也。
有勇健能理决斗讼者，推为大人，无世业相继。
邑落各有小帅，数百千落自为一部。
大人有所召呼，时刻木为信，虽无文字，而部众不敢违犯。
氏姓无常，以大人健者名字为姓。
大人以下，各自蓄牧营产，不相徭役。
其嫁娶则先略女通情，或半岁百日，然后送牛、马、羊畜，以为娉币。
婿随妻还家，妻家无尊卑，旦旦拜之，而不拜其父母。
为妻家仆役，一二年间，妻家乃厚遣送女，居处财物一皆为办。
其俗妻后母，报寡嫂，死则归其故夫。
计谋从用妇人，唯斗战之事乃自决之。
父子男女，相对踞蹲。
以髡头为轻便。
妇人至嫁时乃养发，分为髻，著句决，饰以金碧，犹中国有步摇。
妇人能刺韦作文绣，织氀曷毛。
男子能作弓矢鞍勒，锻金铁为兵器。
其土地宜及东墙。
东墙似蓬草，实如子，至十月而熟。
见鸟兽孕乳，以别四节。
俗贵兵死，敛尸以棺，有哭泣之哀，至葬则歌舞相送。
translation: 
Wuhuan was originally from the Donghu tribe.
In the early Han Dynasty, the Xiongnu Maodun eliminated Donghu, and the remaining people guarded Wuhuan Mountain, so they took Wuhuan as their tribal title.
The custom of Wuhuan is to be good at horseback riding and archery. Wuhuan people are mainly engaged in hunting birds and animals.
The Wuhuan people graze on water and grass and have no fixed residence.
They used felt tents as houses, with doors opening eastward toward the sun.
They ate meat, drank cheese, and made clothes from the hairs of birds and animals.
They attach great importance to young people and despise the elderly. They have a strong temperament and are not open-minded.
When he gets angry, he kills his father and brothers, but never kills his mother, because her mother has her own tribe and her father and brothers have no one to take revenge.
Anyone who is brave and strong and can handle decisive battles and lawsuits is elected as the leader of the tribe. The leader is not inherited from generation to generation.
Each town has its own small leader, and hundreds or thousands of towns form a tribe.
When the tribal leader called for something, he would carve wood as a talisman. Although there was no writing, the tribesmen did not dare to violate it.
The Wuhuan people do not have a fixed surname, and the surname is the name of the strong leader.
From the leader down, people herd livestock and run their own industries, and they did not rely on each other.
In terms of marriage, they first rob the woman and live together, sometimes for half a year or hundreds of days, and then give the woman cattle, horses, sheep and other livestock as betrothal gifts.
When a son-in-law follows his wife back to his parents' home, he pays homage to the people in his wife's family every day, regardless of his superiority or inferiority, but does not pay homage to his wife's parents.
After working as a servant in his wife's house for a year or two, his wife's house gave them many things to send their daughter away, and all daily necessities and belongings were prepared for them.
The custom of the Wuhuan people is to marry their stepmother and have an affair with their widowed sister-in-law. However, after their death, the stepmother and sister-in-law are buried together with their original husbands.
In terms of strategy, they listen to women's opinions, and only make their own decisions when fighting.
Father and son, men and women sit face to face.
They think it is easy to shave off their hair.
Women only grow their hair when they get married, and it is separated and coiled on both sides of the head. The headdress is made of Jujue, which is decorated with gold and jade, just like the Gebuyao in the Central Plains.
Women could embroider leather and weave woolen fabrics.
Men can make bows and arrows, saddles and harnesses, and smelt copper and iron to make weapons.
The boring land is suitable for planting Biaozi and Aunt.
The bunches are like fluffy grass, and the fruits are like rice. They will mature in ten months.
When the Wuhuan people saw the flying black beasts being pregnant and giving birth, they used this to distinguish the four seasons.
Wuhuan's custom is to respect death in battle. They use coffins to collect corpses and have mourning and crying rituals, but when they are buried, they send them off with singing and dancing.

summary:
summary line 0: The Wuhuan originated from the Donghu tribe. After the Xiongnu leader Maodun defeated Donghu in the early Han Dynasty, the survivors took refuge in Wuhuan Mountain and adopted the name as their tribal title. They were skilled horsemen and archers, primarily hunting birds and animals, and practiced nomadic pastoralism without fixed residences. Their felt tents faced east, and they subsisted on meat, cheese, and clothing made from animal hairs. They valued youth over the elderly, had fierce tempers, and would kill fathers and brothers in anger but never mothers, as mothers had their own tribes for revenge. Leaders were chosen based on bravery and strength, not heredity, with towns forming tribes under small leaders. They used carved wood as talismans for commands, lacked writing, and had no fixed surnames, adopting the names of strong leaders instead. Marriage involved abducting women, cohabiting for months, then paying betrothal gifts in livestock. Sons-in-law served their wives' families for one to two years before receiving dowries. They practiced levirate marriage, burying stepmothers and sisters-in-law with their original husbands. Women’s opinions were heeded in strategy, but men decided in battle. They shaved their heads, with women growing hair only after marriage, and wore headdresses decorated with gold and jade. Women embroidered leather and wove wool; men made bows, arrows, saddles, and smelted copper and iron for weapons. They cultivated crops like Biaozi and Aunt, which matured in ten months, and used the pregnancy of black beasts to distinguish seasons. They honored death in battle, used coffins, and conducted mourning rituals with singing and dancing, burning a fat dog with a colored rope along with the deceased’s possessions to guide the soul to Chishan, akin to the Central Plains’ Mount Tai. They sacrificed cattle and sheep to gods, ghosts, heaven, earth, and ancestors, burning the offerings completely. Disobedience to the leader was punishable by death; feuds could be settled with livestock. Wuhuan was located southwest of Dingling and northeast of Wusun, with many vipers.

The Wuhuan people live close to the border. They set out from their felt tents in the morning and arrived at the city at dusk. Every household in the five counties was harmed. Counties were destroyed and people were displaced.
Wuhuan in Baishan, outside the Shanggu border fortress, was the most powerful and wealthy.
In the 21st year of Jianwu's reign, the imperial court sent Fubo General Ma Yuan to lead 3,000 cavalry from Wuruan Pass to attack Wuhuan.
Wuhuan learned the news in advance and all fled one after another. Ma Yuan chased them and killed more than a hundred Wuhuan people and returned to the army.
The Wuhuan people followed Ma Yuan to attack again, but Ma Yuan fled back day and night. By the time he entered the border fortress, more than a thousand horses were dead.
In the 22nd year, the Xiongnu were in civil unrest. Wuhuan took advantage of the Xiongnu's weakness and defeated the Xiongnu. The Xiongnu moved thousands of miles north, and the area south of the desert was completely empty. The emperor bribed Wuhuan with money and cloth.
In the twenty-fifth year, Hao Dan, the leader of Wuhuan in western Liaoning, and other 922 people led his tribe to Fumo and went to the court to pay tribute, offering slaves, cattle and horses, bows and arrows, and the skins of tigers, leopards, and minks.
When he was sent, ethnic minorities from all over came to worship and celebrate in an endless stream. The emperor ordered a grand banquet to be held to express condolences to them and reward them with treasures.
Some Wuhuan people were willing to stay as guards, so the emperor named eighty-one Wuhuan leaders as princes and commanders, letting them live in the fortress and distribute them in the border counties. He asked them to recruit people from their own tribes and provide them with clothing and food. The Wuhuan people then scouted for enemy movements for the Han and helped Mo attack the Xiongnu and Xianbei.
At that time, Situ Peng Ban Biao reported: The Wuhuan people are naturally frivolous and cunning, and like to be bandits. If they are indulged for a long time without anyone to supervise them to surrender and gather, they will definitely invade and plunder the people living there. I am afraid that I will not be able to control them if they are only given to the subordinates who accept the surrender.
I foolishly thought that it would be best to re-establish the Wuhuan captain, which would indeed be beneficial to the Wuhuan people's surrender and gathering, and reduce the country's worries about the border.
The emperor accepted this suggestion. So in Micheng in Shanggu, Wuhuan captains were established again, a military general's office was opened, and Xianbei was also commanded. He also rewarded the sons of the Wuhuan people who were held hostage, and traded with them all year round.
By the time of Emperor Ming, Emperor Zhang, and Emperor He, the Wuhuan people were guarding the frontier and nothing happened.
In the summer of the third year of Emperor An's reign, more than a thousand Wuhuan people from Yuyang and Hu people from Youbeiping invaded Daijun and Shanggu.
In the autumn of this year, Wuhuan of Yanmen led the king Wuhe, together with the Xianbei leader Qiulun and others, and the Guduhou of the Southern Xiongnu, with more than 7,000 cavalry, they invaded Wuyuan and fought with the Wuyuan prefect at Gaoqu Valley in Jiuyuan. The Han army was defeated and the county officials were killed.
The imperial court then sent the cavalry general He Xi, the Liao general Liang Dong and others to attack Wuhe, and they were defeated.
There was no need to beg for surrender, and the Xianbei people fled back beyond the frontier fortress.
From then on, the Wuhuan people gradually became closer to the Han, and the imperial court granted the Wuhuan tribal leader Rong Zhumo the title of pro-Han captaincy.
In the winter of the fourth year of Emperor Shun's reign in Yangjia, Wuhuan invaded Yunzhong and intercepted more than a thousand ox-carts of merchants on the road. Geng Ye, the general of Liao Dynasty, led more than 2,000 people to pursue the Wuhuan people. The battle was unfavorable, and they fought again in Shanan, killing 500 people.
The Wuhuan people then surrounded Geng Ye in Lanchi City. The imperial court then mobilized 2,000 trace-seeking soldiers and 1,000 soldiers from the Duliao camp to cooperate with the garrison in Shangjun to conquer Wuhuan, and the Wuhuan people retreated.
In the fifth year of Yonghe, Wuhuan's tribal leaders Ajian, Qiangqu and others and Julongwusi from the left of the southern Xiongnu rebelled. Zhang Dan, the central general, defeated and killed them, and the rest all surrendered.
During the Yongshou period of Emperor Huan, the Wuhuan people in Shuofang rebelled with Xiuzhu Tuge. Zhonglang attacked Zhang Huan and pacified the enemy.
In the summer of the ninth year of Yanxi, Wuhuan, together with Xianbei and the Southern Xiongnu, invaded nine counties along the border. They rebelled at the same time. Zhang Huan conquered the enemy, and they all left the border fortress.
In the early years of Emperor Ling, there was a tribal leader named Nanlou in Shanggu, who had more than 9,000 tribes of tribes, and there was a leader named Qiu Liju in western Liaoning, with more than 5,000 tribes of tribes, and they all called themselves kings. In addition, Su Puyan in Liaodong had more than a thousand tribes and called himself King Qiao; Wuyan in Youbeiping had more than 800 tribes and called himself King Hanlu: they were both brave and strong and had many strategies.
In the fourth year of Chongchuo, the governor of Protozoa Mountain rebelled. He joined Qiu Liju's tribe and proclaimed himself King Mitian Anding. He then became the marshal of the Wuhuan people in each county and invaded and plundered the four prefectures of Qingzhou, Xuzhou, Youzhou and Jizhou.
In the fifth year, the imperial court appointed Liu Yu as the shepherd of Youzhou. Liu Yu offered a reward for recruiting people to cut off Zhang Chun's head, and the northern states were pacified.
During the Chuping reign of Emperor Xian, Qiu Liju died. His son Lou Ban was young, and his nephew Tadun, who knew how to use troops and had strategies, succeeded Lou Ban and took charge of the Wuhuan people in three counties. All the people obeyed his orders.
In the early years of Jian'an, Yuan Shao, the shepherd of Jizhou, was at loggerheads with former general Gongsun Zan. Tadun sent an envoy to Yuan Shao to request peace, and then sent troops to help Yuan Shao attack Gongsun Zan and defeated Gongsun Zan.
In the name of the imperial court, Yuan Shao granted Tadun, Nanlou, Su Puyan, Wuyan and others the Chanyu seal and ribbon.
Later, Nanlou and Su Puyan led their troops to support Louban as Chanyu and Tadun as king, but Tadun was still responsible for making suggestions.
Yan Rou from Guangyang was trapped in Wuhuan and Xianbei since childhood, and was attached and trusted by the Wuhuan and Xianbei people. Yan Rou then used the Xianbei tribe to kill Wuhuan captain Xing Ju and replace him.
Yuan Shao favored Yan Rou and comforted her, so as to stabilize the northern border.
When Yuan Shao's son Yuan Shang failed, he defected to Tadun.

The Wuhuan people live close to the border. They set out from their felt tents in the morning and arrived at the city at dusk. Every household in the five counties was harmed. Counties were destroyed and people were displaced.
Wuhuan in Baishan, outside the Shanggu border fortress, was the most powerful and wealthy.
In the 21st year of Jianwu's reign, the imperial court sent Fubo General Ma Yuan to lead 3,000 cavalry from Wuruan Pass to attack Wuhuan.
Wuhuan learned the news in advance and all fled one after another. Ma Yuan chased them and killed more than a hundred Wuhuan people and returned to the army.
The Wuhuan people followed Ma Yuan to attack again, but Ma Yuan fled back day and night. By the time he entered the border fortress, more than a thousand horses were dead.
In the 22nd year, the Xiongnu were in civil unrest. Wuhuan took advantage of the Xiongnu's weakness and defeated the Xiongnu. The Xiongnu moved thousands of miles north, and the area south of the desert was completely empty. The emperor bribed Wuhuan with money and cloth.
In the twenty-fifth year, Hao Dan, the leader of Wuhuan in western Liaoning, and other 922 people led his tribe to Fumo and went to the court to pay tribute, offering slaves, cattle and horses, bows and arrows, and the skins of tigers, leopards, and minks.
When he was sent, ethnic minorities from all over came to worship and celebrate in an endless stream. The emperor ordered a grand banquet to be held to express condolences to them and reward them with treasures.
Some Wuhuan people were willing to stay as guards, so the emperor named eighty-one Wuhuan leaders as princes and commanders, letting them live in the fortress and distribute them in the border counties. He asked them to recruit people from their own tribes and provide them with clothing and food. The Wuhuan people then scouted for enemy movements for the Han and helped Mo attack the Xiongnu and Xianbei.
At that time, Situ Peng Ban Biao reported: The Wuhuan people are naturally frivolous and cunning, and like to be bandits. If they are indulged for a long time without anyone to supervise them to surrender and gather, they will definitely invade and plunder the people living there. I am afraid that I will not be able to control them if they are only given to the subordinates who accept the surrender.
I foolishly thought that it would be best to re-establish the Wuhuan captain, which would indeed be beneficial to the Wuhuan people's surrender and gathering, and reduce the country's worries about the border.
The emperor accepted this suggestion. So in Micheng in Shanggu, Wuhuan captains were established again, a military general's office was opened, and Xianbei was also commanded. He also rewarded the sons of the Wuhuan people who were held hostage, and traded with them all year round.
By the time of Emperor Ming, Emperor Zhang, and Emperor He, the Wuhuan people were guarding the frontier and nothing happened.
In the summer of the third year of Emperor An's reign, more than a thousand Wuhuan people from Yuyang and Hu people from Youbeiping invaded Daijun and Shanggu.
In the autumn of this year, Wuhuan of Yanmen led the king Wuhe, together with the Xianbei leader Qiulun and others, and the Guduhou of the Southern Xiongnu, with more than 7,000 cavalry, they invaded Wuyuan and fought with the Wuyuan prefect at Gaoqu Valley in Jiuyuan. The Han army was defeated and the county officials were killed.
The imperial court then sent the cavalry general He Xi, the Liao general Liang Dong and others to attack Wuhe, and they were defeated.
There was no need to beg for surrender, and the Xianbei people fled back beyond the frontier fortress.
From then on, the Wuhuan people gradually became closer to the Han, and the imperial court granted the Wuhuan tribal leader Rong Zhumo the title of pro-Han captaincy.
In the winter of the fourth year of Emperor Shun's reign in Yangjia, Wuhuan invaded Yunzhong and intercepted more than a thousand ox-carts of merchants on the road. Geng Ye, the general of Liao Dynasty, led more than 2,000 people to pursue the Wuhuan people. The battle was unfavorable, and they fought again in Shanan, killing 500 people.
The Wuhuan people then surrounded Geng Ye in Lanchi City. The imperial court then mobilized 2,000 trace-seeking soldiers and 1,000 soldiers from the Duliao camp to cooperate with the garrison in Shangjun to conquer Wuhuan, and the Wuhuan people retreated.
In the fifth year of Yonghe, Wuhuan's tribal leaders Ajian, Qiangqu and others and Julongwusi from the left of the southern Xiongnu rebelled. Zhang Dan, the central general, defeated and killed them, and the rest all surrendered.
During the Yongshou period of Emperor Huan, the Wuhuan people in Shuofang rebelled with Xiuzhu Tuge. Zhonglang attacked Zhang Huan and pacified the enemy.
In the summer of the ninth year of Yanxi, Wuhuan, together with Xianbei and the Southern Xiongnu, invaded nine counties along the border. They rebelled at the same time. Zhang Huan conquered the enemy, and they all left the border fortress.
In the early years of Emperor Ling, there was a tribal leader named Nanlou in Shanggu, who had more than 9,000 tribes of tribes, and there was a leader named Qiu Liju in western Liaoning, with more than 5,000 tribes of tribes, and they all called themselves kings. In addition, Su Puyan in Liaodong had more than a thousand tribes and called himself King Qiao; Wuyan in Youbeiping had more than 800 tribes and called himself King Hanlu: they were both brave and strong and had many strategies.
In the fourth year of Chongchuo, the governor of Protozoa Mountain rebelled. He joined Qiu Liju's tribe and proclaimed himself King Mitian Anding. He then became the marshal of the Wuhuan people in each county and invaded and plundered the four prefectures of Qingzhou, Xuzhou, Youzhou and Jizhou.
In the fifth year, the imperial court appointed Liu Yu as the shepherd of Youzhou. Liu Yu offered a reward for recruiting people to cut off Zhang Chun's head, and the northern states were pacified.
During the Chuping reign of Emperor Xian, Qiu Liju died. His son Lou Ban was young, and his nephew Tadun, who knew how to use troops and had strategies, succeeded Lou Ban and took charge of the Wuhuan people in three counties. All the people obeyed his orders.
In the early years of Jian'an, Yuan Shao, the shepherd of Jizhou, was at loggerheads with former general Gongsun Zan. Tadun sent an envoy to Yuan Shao to request peace, and then sent troops to help Yuan Shao attack Gongsun Zan and defeated Gongsun Zan.
In the name of the imperial court, Yuan Shao granted Tadun, Nanlou, Su Puyan, Wuyan and others the Chanyu seal and ribbon.
Later, Nanlou and Su Puyan led their troops to support Louban as Chanyu and Tadun as king, but Tadun was still responsible for making suggestions.
Yan Rou from Guangyang was trapped in Wuhuan and Xianbei since childhood, and was attached and trusted by the Wuhuan and Xianbei people. Yan Rou then used the Xianbei tribe to kill Wuhuan captain Xing Ju and replace him.
Yuan Shao favored Yan Rou and comforted her, so as to stabilize the northern border.
When Yuan Shao's son Yuan Shang failed, he defected to Tadun.
