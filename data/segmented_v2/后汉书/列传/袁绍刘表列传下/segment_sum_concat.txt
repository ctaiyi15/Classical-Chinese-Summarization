Liu Biao was more than eight feet tall, with a gentle appearance and a burly build.
Together with Zhang Jian and others from the same county, he was slandered and discussed as "Ba Gu". The emperor issued an edict to arrest the party members and punish them, but Liu Biao escaped without being caught.
After the party ban was lifted, Liu Biao was summoned by General He Jin as an assistant.
In the first year of Chuping, Sun Jian, the governor of Changsha, killed Wang Xu, the governor of Jingzhou, and issued an edict appointing Liu Biao as the governor of Jingzhou.
At that time, there were many bandits of the same clan in the south of the Yangtze River, and Yuan Shu's troops were stationed in Luyang to block the road. Liu Biao could not reach Jingzhou, so he rode into Yicheng alone and invited Kuai Yue from Nanjun and Cai Mao from Xiangyang to discuss countermeasures.
Liu Biao said to Kuai Yue: Although there are many thieves in the same clan, no one will join them. If Yuan Jie takes advantage of them, disaster will inevitably come.
I want to recruit troops, but I'm worried that I can't combine them. What strategy should I use?
Kuai Yue replied: When governing peace, benevolence and righteousness should be the first priority; when governing war, the first priority should be to use contingency strategies.

summary:
Liu Biao, courtesy name Jingsheng, was a descendant of King Lu Gong from Gaoping, Shanyang. Over eight feet tall with a gentle appearance and burly build, he was implicated in the "Ba Gu" party affair and escaped arrest. After the party ban was lifted, he served as an assistant to General He Jin. In the first year of Chuping, he was appointed Governor of Jingzhou following Sun Jian's murder of Governor Wang Xu. Unable to reach Jingzhou due to bandits and Yuan Shu's troops blocking the road, Liu Biao rode alone to Yicheng and consulted Kuai Yue and Cai Mao. Following Kuai Yue's advice, he lured and killed fifteen clan bandit leaders, then pacified their tribes. The Jiangxia thieves Zhang Hu and Chen Li surrendered after negotiations, and the region south of the Yangtze River was subdued. Liu Biao organized troops to observe the situation. Yuan Shu and Sun Jian attacked Liu Biao, but Sun Jian was killed by a stray arrow during the siege of Xiangyang. Liu Biao sent tribute to Emperor Xian and was appointed General of Zhennan, Shepherd of Jingzhou, and Marquis of Wu. When Zhang Ji attacked Lucheng and was killed, Liu Biao accepted condolences and recruited Zhang Ji's troops. He suppressed rebellions by Zhang Xian and others, expanding his territory to connect with Wuling in the south and Han in the north, commanding over 100,000 armored warriors. Liu Biao pacified bandits, supported scholars, established schools, and commissioned commentaries on the Five Classics.
