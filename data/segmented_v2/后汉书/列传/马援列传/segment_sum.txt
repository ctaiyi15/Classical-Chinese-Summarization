His ancestor Zhao She was a general of Zhao, with the title of Ma Fujun, and his descendants considered him a surname.
During the reign of Emperor Wu, he moved two thousand stones from Handan to Maoling.
Ma Tong, his great-grandfather, was granted the title of Chonghehou due to his meritorious service. His brother Ho Luo was implicated in the rebellion and was killed, so Ma Yuan's grandfather and father were not allowed to be prominent officials.
Ma Yuan's three elder brothers, Ma Kuang, Ma Yu, and Ma Yuan, were all talented. At the time of Wang Mang, they were all worth two thousand shi.
Ma Yuan was orphaned when he was twelve years old. He was young and ambitious, and his brothers thought he was strange.
He once taught him to learn Qi poetry, but Ma Yuan could not stick to the chapters and sentences, so he said goodbye to his brother Ma Kuang and wanted to farm and herd in the border counties.
Ma Kuang said: You have great talent, and you will be able to succeed later in the evening.
Good craftsmen don't tell people to use big wood, but just follow your preferences.
On the first anniversary of Ma Kuang's death, Ma Yuan wore mourning clothes and did not leave the tomb. He respected his widowed sister-in-law and would not enter the cottage until he had good hair and a good hat.
Later, he became the postal supervisor of the county and sent prisoners to Siming Mansion. The prisoners had serious crimes, so Ma Yuan took pity on him and let him go, then fled to the north.
After he was pardoned, he left his livestock behind. Many of his guests joined him, and hundreds of families joined him.
When he traveled to Longhan, he often said to his guests: A man's ambition should be stronger when he is poor and more heroic when he is old.
Because they are engaged in farming and grazing, they have thousands of cattle, horses and sheep, and tens of thousands of grains.
Then he sighed: Any property obtained from farming, animal husbandry and commerce is valuable because it can help others, otherwise you will be nothing more than a miser!
So he distributed all his property to his elder brother and his old friend. Living in sheepskin pants.
At the end of Wang Mang's reign, armies from all directions rose up. Wang Mang recruited heroes from his younger brother, General Wang Linguang. He recruited Ma Yuan and Yuan She from the same county as couriers and recommended them to Wang Mang.
Wang Mang took Yuanshe as the great Yin of Zhenrong, and Ma Yuan as the new great Yin.
When Wang Mang was defeated, Ma Yuan's brother Ma Yuan left the county with Ma Yuan to lead the Zengshan Company, and fled to Liangzhou.
When Shizu ascended the throne, the horsemen went to Luoyang first. When the emperor sent the horsemen back, they were still led by Zengshan Company and died in the official position.
Ma Yuan therefore stayed in Xizhou. Kuai Xiao respected him very much, regarded him as General Suide, and made joint decisions with him.
At this time Gongsun Shu proclaimed himself emperor in Shu, and Wei Xiao sent Ma Yuan to Shu to observe.
Ma Yuan and Gongsun Shu were from the same hometown, and they had always been good friends. He thought that Gongsun Shu would shake hands with him after he left, and he would be as happy as before. But this time Gongsun Shu showed his favor to the imperial guards and invited Ma Yuan to meet him. After paying homage, he took Ma Yuan to the residence, made Ma Yuan cloth clothes for him, gave him a hat, met with all the officials in the ancestral temple, and set up his old friend's seat.

summary:
summary line 2: Ma Yuan served as Governor of Longxi, where he focused on spreading favor and trust, appointing capable officials, and encouraging agriculture and animal husbandry. He successfully pacified the region and proposed minting five-baht coins, which was implemented. He later led campaigns against the Wuxi barbarians in Wuling, but died of illness at age 62. After his death, he was falsely accused of bringing back treasures from the south, leading to the emperor's anger and the revocation of his marquis title. His family was disgraced until a letter from Zhu Bo, a former associate, helped clear his name. Ma Yuan's daughter later became empress, and he was posthumously honored as Marquis Zhongcheng. His sons included Ma Liao, Ma Fang, Ma Guang, and Ma Keqing, the latter dying young. The historian notes Ma Yuan's rise to fame and his tragic fall due to slander, reflecting on the perils of fame and the importance of self-reflection.
