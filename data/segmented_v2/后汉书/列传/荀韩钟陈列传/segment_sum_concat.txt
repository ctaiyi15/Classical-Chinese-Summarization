summary:
**Xun Shuang (Ciming)** was eager to learn from childhood, understanding "Spring and Autumn" and "The Analects" by age twelve. Taiwei Du Qiao praised him as capable of being a teacher. Xun Shuang delved deeply into the scriptures, ignoring village celebrations and funerals. In the ninth year of Yanxi, he was recommended as filial and incorruptible, becoming a doctor. In response to an imperial question, he discussed the Han Dynasty's virtue of fire, filial piety, and the importance of proper mourning rites. He criticized officials who failed to observe three-year mourning periods and advocated for correcting funeral etiquette. He also addressed the improper subordination of husbands to wives in marriage rituals, arguing it violated cosmic principles. He condemned the excessive number of palace women—five to six thousand—which drained the treasury and caused disasters, urging their release to restore harmony. He further criticized ministers who usurped imperial privileges in dress and luxury. After submitting his memorial, he abandoned his post. Later, due to conflicts with eunuchs, he lived in seclusion for over ten years, writing books and becoming a great Confucian. After the party ban was lifted, he was recruited multiple times but refused. When Yuan Feng died, Xun Shuang mourned him for three years, setting a custom. He later served under General He Jin and then Dong Zhuo, rising to Sikong within ninety-five days. Seeing Dong Zhuo's cruelty, he conspired with Wang Yun but died of illness at sixty-three. He wrote numerous works, including "Li," "Yizhuan," and "Hanyu," totaling over a hundred chapters, many now lost. **Xun Yue (Zhongyu)** was the son of Xun Jian, who died early. During Emperor Ling's reign, when eunuchs held power, he feigned illness and lived in seclusion. He later served under Cao Cao and became Minister of Huangmen. Emperor Xian, fond of literature, conversed with Xun Yue and Kong Rong daily. As political power shifted to Cao Cao, Xun Yue wrote five chapters of "Shenjian" on governance, emphasizing benevolence and righteousness. He identified four troubles—hypocrisy, selfishness, debauchery, and luxury—and five policies: promoting agriculture, distinguishing good from evil, spreading culture, establishing military force, and enforcing rewards and punishments. He also criticized the marriage system for placing women above men and advocated for historical recording. The emperor, finding Bangu's "Hanshu" too lengthy, ordered Xun Yue to write thirty chapters of "Han Ji" in the style of "Zuo Shi Zhuan." Xun Yue's work was concise yet detailed. He also wrote "Chongde" and "Zhenglun." He died in the fourteenth year of Jian'an at age sixty-two.

Although everyone in the county went, none of the famous scholar-bureaucrats went. Zhang Rang felt that he had no face and was very ashamed, but Chen Chen went to mourn alone.
Later, the party members were killed again. Zhang Rang was grateful to Chen Chen for his condolences. Many people were forgiven and their wealth and lives were saved.
In the countryside, Chen Chen set an example by being fair.
If there is a dispute, he will seek correct judgment and the right and wrong will be explained clearly. After returning home, there will be no complaints.
Some even sighed and said: I would rather be punished than be told wrong by Chen Chen. At this time, there was a famine and the people were in poverty. A thief entered his room and stopped on the beam.
Chen Shi secretly saw it, so he got up, arranged the bedding, and cleaned the house. Then he called his descendants together and warned him sternly: "People must encourage themselves. Bad people are not necessarily inherently bad. Habits are caused by nature, leading to this point." Liang Shangjun is this kind of person!
The thief was shocked. He knelt on the ground and knelt down to apologize. Chen Chen slowly told him: "You don't look like a bad person. You should reflect deeply and become a good person."
However, this time it was probably because of poverty that he did this. He ordered his family to send him two pieces of silk.
Since then, there have been no burglaries in the county.
Every time Taiwei Yang Ci and Situ Chen Dan were appointed ministers, hundreds of officials came to congratulate them. Yang Ci and others often lamented that Chen Chen had not ascended to the big position, and they felt ashamed. When the party ban was lifted, Generals He Jin and Situ Yuan Wei sent people to persuade Chen Chen, hoping to make an exception and give Chen Chen a big position.
Chen Chen declined the envoy and said: I have not thought about the things in the world for a long time. I am just dressing up my clothes and waiting for death. At this time, every time the three princes were vacant, everyone thought of Chen Chen. He was called up many times and refused to go. He closed the door and hung up the car to rest and retire.
He died at home in the fourth year of Zhongping at the age of eighty-four.
He Jin sent envoys to offer condolences, and more than 30,000 people from all over the country came to express their condolences, hundreds of them wearing mourning clothes.
They jointly carved stones and erected monuments, and their posthumous title was Mr. Wen Fan.
He has six sons, among whom Chen Ji and Chen Chen are the best.
Chen Ji's character Yuan Fang was also praised by others for his virtuous conduct.
His brothers are filial, his women are harmonious, and the younger generations in the village are envious of his good family tradition.
Being imprisoned by the party, he worked hard to write a book of tens of thousands of words called "Chen Zi".
The ban on the party was lifted, and the four governments competed for appointments, but he was not accepted.
When his father passed away, he was extremely grieved. He often vomited blood and died. Although the period of mourning was over, he was still grieving so much that his body became emaciated, leaving only a few bones, and he became like a dead person.
The governor of Yuzhou believed that Chen Ji's excellence was extremely valuable, so he commissioned it as a minister, and all cities in Yuzhou painted images of Chen Ji and other figures to encourage customs.
summary:
**Chen Chen (Zhonggong)** was from Xu County, Yingchuan, of humble origin. From childhood, he was respected by peers. He served as a county official and studied at Taixue. When falsely accused of murder, he was tortured but released. Later, as postal supervisor, he treated his accuser with courtesy, earning admiration. As a meritorious officer, he refused to appoint an unsuitable candidate recommended by eunuch Hou Lan, taking the blame himself. He served as Chief of Taiqiu, governing with virtue and quietness. During the party arrests, he willingly imprisoned himself. He mourned for Zhang Rang's father, later saving many lives during renewed party persecutions. In his village, he settled disputes fairly. When a thief entered his home, he admonished him and gave him silk, ending burglaries in the county. He repeatedly refused high positions, closing his door to retire. He died at eighty-four in the fourth year of Zhongping. Over 30,000 mourners attended his funeral. His posthumous title was Mr. Wen Fan. His sons Chen Ji and Chen Chen were also virtuous. Chen Ji served under Dong Zhuo but opposed moving the capital, warning of destruction. He later became Dahonglu and died at seventy-one. Historians praised Chen Chen's virtue, noting that while imperial teachings declined, integrity survived among the people.
