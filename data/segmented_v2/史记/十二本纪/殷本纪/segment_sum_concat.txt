周武王遂斩纣头，县之白旗。

translation: 
When Xibo attacked the hungry country and destroyed it, Zuyi, the minister of Zhou, heard about it and resented Zhou. He was very frightened and went to Zhou to report: God has cut off the longevity of our Yin country. People who know good and bad luck use big tortoises for divination, but there are no good omens. It’s not that the late king didn’t protect our descendants, but that you, the great king, were licentious and tyrannical and cut yourself off from heaven. Therefore, God abandoned us, so that we can’t live in peace of mind. If we don’t understand God’s will, we can’t follow the ordinary laws. As a result, the people of our country all hope that the Yin Kingdom will perish. They say: Why doesn’t God show its majesty? Why haven’t God’s orders come yet?
Your Majesty, what do you want to do now?
Zhou said: Wasn't it that I was blessed by heaven when I was born?
After Zuyi returned home, he said: Zhou can no longer be persuaded!
After the death of Xibochang, King Wu of Zhou led his army to the east. When they arrived at Mengjin, the princes betrayed Yin and Zhou and came to join King Wu from eight hundred countries.
The princes all said: It's time to attack Zhou!
King Wu of Zhou said: You don’t know the destiny of heaven.
So the class teacher returned to China.
Zhou became even more promiscuous and had no end.
Wei Zi repeatedly advised Zhou, but Zhou refused to listen. Wei Zi discussed with the Grand Master and the Young Master and left Yin.
Bigan said: As a minister, I have to risk my life to advise.
So he tried his best to persuade Zhou.
Zhou was furious and said: I heard that the saint's heart has seven holes.
So he cut open Bigan's chest and took out his heart to see.
Jizi was frightened when he saw it, so he pretended to be crazy and became a slave. Zhou imprisoned Jizi again.
The grand masters and young masters of Yin State took the sacrificial vessels and musical instruments and fled to Zhou State in a hurry.
King Wu of Zhou led the princes to attack Yin Zhou.
Zhou also sent troops to resist the Zhou army in Muye.
On the day of Jiazi, Zhou's army was defeated. Zhou fled into the city, climbed to the deer platform, put on his precious jade clothes, ran to the fire and burned himself to death.
King Wu of Zhou cut off Zhou's head and hung it on a white flagpole.
King Zhou Wu executed Daji again, released the imprisoned Jizi, repaired Bigan's tomb, and commended Shang Rong's alleys.
He also enfeoffed Zhou's son Wu Geng and his father, allowing him to continue to worship Yin's ancestors and order him to implement Pan Geng's virtuous government. The people of Yin were very happy.
So King Wu of Zhou became the emperor.
Because descendants of the Yin and Shang dynasties devalued the title of emperor, he was called king.
King Wu of Zhou made the descendants of Yin princes and subordinated them to Zhou.
After the death of King Wu of Zhou, Wu Geng, Uncle Guan, and Uncle Cai launched a rebellion. King Cheng of Zhou ordered Zhou Gongdan to kill them, and made Wei Zi the king of the Song Dynasty to continue the incense of the Yin Dynasty.

summary:
After Wu Ding's death, Zu Geng and Zu Jia ruled, with Zu Jia's promiscuity causing decline. Subsequent rulers like Lin Xin, Geng Ding, and Wu Yi saw further decline. Wu Yi's cruelty, including making a puppet and shooting a blood-filled pouch, led to his death by lightning. His son Tai Ding and then Yi ruled, with Yi's son Xin (Zhou) succeeding. Zhou was talented but tyrannical, indulging in debauchery with Daji, increasing taxes, and inventing the Pao Ge torture. He killed Jiu Hou and E Hou, imprisoned Xibo (later King Wen), who was released after bribes. Xibo practiced virtuous governance, attracting princes. Zhou's minister Bigan was killed for advising, and Jizi was imprisoned. King Wu of Zhou led a revolt, defeated Zhou at Muye, and Zhou burned himself to death. King Wu executed Daji, released Jizi, and enfeoffed Zhou's son Wu Geng. Later, Wu Geng rebelled, and King Cheng made Wei Zi the king of Song to continue Yin's lineage.
