孝宣皇帝，武帝曾孙，戾太子孙也。
太子纳史良娣，生史皇孙。皇孙纳王夫人，生宣帝，号曰皇曾孙。
生数月，遭巫蛊事，太子、良娣、皇孙、王夫人皆遇害。
语在《太子传》。
曾孙虽在襁褓，犹坐收系郡邸狱。
而邴吉为廷尉监，治巫蛊于郡邸，怜曾孙之亡辜，使女徒复作淮阳赵征卿、渭城胡组更乳养，私给衣食，视遇甚有恩。
巫蛊事连岁不决。
至后元二年，武帝疾，往来长杨、五柞宫，望气者言长安狱中有天子气，上遣使者分条中都官狱系者，轻、重皆杀之。
内谒者令郭穰夜至郡邸狱，吉拒闭，使者不得入，曾孙赖吉得全。
因遭大赦，吉乃载曾孙送祖母史良娣家。
语在吉及外戚《传》。
后有诏掖庭养视，上属籍宗正。
时掖庭令张贺尝事戾太子，思顾旧恩，哀曾孙，奉养甚谨，以私钱供给教书。
既壮，为取暴室啬夫许广汉女。曾孙因依倚广汉兄弟及祖母家史氏。
受《诗》于东海澓中翁，高材好学，然亦喜游侠，斗鸡走马，具知闾里奸邪，吏治得失。
数上下诸陵，周遍三辅，常困于莲勺卤中。
尤乐杜、鄠之间，率常在下杜。
时会朝请，舍长安尚冠里，身足下有毛，卧居数有光耀。每买饼，所从买家辄大雠，亦以是自怪。
元平元年四月，昭帝崩，毋嗣。
大将军霍光请皇后征昌邑王。
六月丙寅，王受皇帝玺、绶，尊皇后曰皇太后。
translation: 
Emperor Xuan of the Han Dynasty, great-grandson of Emperor Wu, and grandson of Prince Ru.
Prince Ru took in Shi Liangdi and gave birth to Emperor Shi's grandson. His grandson took in Mrs. Wang and gave birth to Emperor Xuan, who was named Zenghuang's grandson.
A few months after giving birth, a "witchcraft incident" occurred. The prince, Liang Di, the emperor's grandson, and Mrs. Wang were all killed.
The facts are all recorded in "The Legend of the Prince".
Even though his great-grandson was still an infant, he was imprisoned.
At that time, Bingji was an official of Tingwei and was responsible for handling witchcraft incidents. He was extremely sympathetic to his great-grandson's innocent misfortune, so he ordered the female convicts Zhao Zhengqing from Huaiyang and Hu Zu from Weicheng to exchange breast-feeding and provide them with food and clothing privately. They treated him very kindly.
The witchcraft incident could not be concluded for several years.
In the second year of the Yuan Dynasty, Emperor Wu was ill and traveled back and forth between Changyang and Wuzha palaces. The astrologers who were watching the clouds said that there was the emperor's energy in Chang'an prison. Emperor Wu sent envoys to inform the various officials in the capital that criminals imprisoned due to witchcraft incidents would be executed regardless of their severity.
The emissary ordered Guo Rang to go to the county prison at night for inspection. Bingji closed the prison door so that the envoys could not enter, but his great-grandson Lai Bingji was saved.
Later, when there was a general amnesty, Bingji drove his great-grandson to his grandmother Shi Liangdi's home.
This incident is recorded in "Biography of Bingji" and "Biography of Foreign Relatives".
Later, there was an edict, sending officials from Yeting to visit the great-grandson at the historian's home, and ordered Zongzheng to record him in the royal genealogy.
At this time, Zhang He, the order of Yeting, was Prince Li's former attendant. He missed Prince Li's old kindness and sympathized with his great-grandson, so he treated his great-grandson considerately and used his personal money to support his great-grandson's education.
When he was older, he married the daughter of Xu Guanghan, a court dyer, for his great-grandson. Therefore, his great-grandson relied on the Guanghan brothers and the Shi family of his grandmother's family.
He returned from the East China Sea to learn "Poetry" from Zhong Weng. He was talented and never tired of learning. He also had the character of drawing a sword to help when the road was rough. He fought chickens and horses and made many friends. He was familiar with the evil in the alleys and the gains and losses of official governance.
He visited the tombs of his ancestors many times to pay his respects, and went to the Sanfu District of Kyoto to understand the people's sentiments. He was once trapped and humiliated in the salt pond of Lianshao County, especially in Du County?
He traveled between counties, mostly in Xiadu.
He went to the imperial court to attend court invitations and lived in Shangguanli, Chang'an. He had hair all over his body and on his feet. He always glowed when sleeping. Every time he went to a bakery to buy cakes, the business of the bakery prospered. People were surprised by this.
In April of the first year of Yuanping, Emperor Zhao died without any heirs.
General Huo Guang asked the queen to summon the king of Changyi to be the emperor.
On June 1, the king of Changyi received the emperor's jade seal and imperial robes, and honored the queen as the empress dowager.

summary:
summary line 0: Emperor Xuan, great-grandson of Emperor Wu, was born into tragedy. His grandfather Prince Ru, grandmother Shi Liangdi, and parents were all killed in the "witchcraft incident" when he was an infant. Imprisoned, he was saved by official Bingji, who arranged for female convicts to nurse him. After a general amnesty, Bingji took him to his grandmother's home. Later, Yeting official Zhang He, remembering his loyalty to Prince Li, supported the boy's education and arranged his marriage to Xu Guanghan's daughter.

The edict states: The Great Sima General Bo Luhou has been with Emperor Xiaowu for more than thirty years, and has assisted Emperor Xiaozhao for more than ten years. He has been loyal to the country many times, and led the Sangong, Jiuqing, officials and other ministers to formulate long-term peace and stability strategies to stabilize the ancestral temple and bring peace to the people of the world.
I deeply admire him for his high merit and virtue. Now let his descendants enjoy his glory.
If his nobility is not reduced, if he does not bear the burden of corvee and tax from generation to generation, his merits will be like that of Prime Minister Xiao He.
In the fourth month of summer, phoenixes gathered in Lu County, followed by flocks of birds.
Amnesty to the world.
In the fifth month, Pingqiuhou Wang Qian, the official of Guanglu, was found guilty and sentenced to death in prison.
The emperor began to take charge of the government personally, and missed the merits of the general Huo Guang, so he appointed Huo Guang's nephew, Lepinghou Huoshan, to be the secretary and take charge of the government affairs. He also ordered the ministers to report their work to the emperor frequently so that the emperor could grasp the situation. He would listen to work reports every five days and perform duties in various departments below the prime minister. To describe their situation and measures, and to test their political achievements and abilities.
The ministers should be promoted and those who have made special contributions should be generously rewarded so that their duties can be passed down to their descendants and their positions will not be changed.
This resulted in the imperial court having complete facilities and adequate staffing. The top and bottom are of one heart and one mind, and there is no intention of settling down.
In the spring and March of the third year, an edict was issued saying: It has been said that meritorious deeds are not rewarded, and crimes are not punished. Even the emperors of the Tang Dynasty, Yao, Yu and Shun could not transform the world with virtue.
Today, Wang Cheng, the Prime Minister of Jiaodong, warmly receives and properly resettles the refugees, so that more than 80,000 refugees have registered their registered residence in Jiaodong. His political achievements are different from those of ordinary people. Now Wang Cheng's salary has been raised to two thousand dan and he has been given the title of Marquis of Guannei.
The edict also said: The widowed, lonely, elderly and poor people are the people I have the most sympathy and concern for.
In the past, edicts were issued to lend public fields, seeds, and grain rations.
Now I will give silk to the widowed and lonely old man.
Erqianshi officials should strictly require their staff to pay serious attention to them and not let them lose their regular jobs.
Let the Zhongyuan County appoint virtuous and upright people who can go deep among the people and understand the people's sentiments.
On the 22nd day of the fourth lunar month in the summer, the Crown Prince was established and the whole country was granted amnesty.
He was given the title of Marquis of Guannei, the imperial censor, and the right concubine of the middle two thousand stone ranks. As his father's heir, he was promoted to the first rank in the world.
King Guangling was rewarded with a thousand catties of gold, each of the fifteen princes and kings was given a hundred catties of gold each, and the eighty-seven people in the feudal kingdom were each rewarded with twenty catties of gold.

summary:

He rewarded meritorious officials generously, including Huo Guang, Yang Chang, and others. He issued edicts promoting agriculture, reducing waste, and caring for the poor. When earthquakes struck, he wore plain clothes, avoided the main hall, and sought advice from officials.
