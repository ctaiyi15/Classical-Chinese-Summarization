It's just that I think my body is disabled and I am in a lowly position. I will be criticized for my slightest actions. Subjectively I want to make a difference, but objectively it will cause harm. Therefore, I am depressed and can't find a close friend to talk to. As the saying goes: For whom do I do this, and who will listen to me?
Therefore, when Zhong Ziqi died, Boya stopped playing the harp for the rest of his life.
Why?
Because a gentleman works for those who know him; a woman dresses for those who love her.
A physically disabled person like me, even if I have valuable talents like Suihou Pearl and He Shibi, and noble moral character like Xu You and Boyi, I cannot be proud of it. On the contrary, I will be ridiculed by others and make myself even more filthy.
I should have replied to you a long time ago. I just returned from the emperor's eastward tour, and I am busy with trivial matters. The days we see each other are getting fewer and fewer, and I am in a hurry and have no time to explain my feelings in detail.
Now you are in catastrophe, and the execution date will be close in a month. I will follow the emperor to Yongdi again. I am afraid that you will encounter misfortune in the blink of an eye.
In this way, it will be impossible for me to express my inner resentment to you, and your soul under the Nine Springs will also hold endless hatred.
Please allow me to share my humble opinion.
I apologize for not replying to you for a long time.

Only when scholars possess these five things can they gain a foothold in society and enter the ranks of gentlemen.
summary:
Sima Qian wrote a letter to his friend Ren An, explaining his reasons for enduring humiliation rather than committing suicide. He emphasized the importance of completing his historical work, comparing himself to ancient figures like King Wen, Confucius, Qu Yuan, and others who wrote under duress. He argued that death could be heavier than a mountain or lighter than a feather, and that his duty was to finish his book to express his thoughts and preserve history. After his death, his works gradually spread, and his grandson Yang Pang helped publish them. The text concludes with a commentary praising Sima Qian as a talented historian but noting his deviations from orthodox views, such as favoring Huang-Lao over the Six Classics and promoting knights and wealth.
