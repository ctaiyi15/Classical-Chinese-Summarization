summary line 0: Xue Xuan, a native of Tan County, Shuhai, rose from minor official posts to become a respected administrator. Known for his integrity and legal expertise, he served as Censor Zhongcheng under Emperor Cheng, advocating for simpler governance and criticizing harsh laws that disrupted social harmony. His memorials urged leniency and moral cultivation, which the emperor approved. As governor of Linhuai and later Chenliu, he quelled bandits and earned widespread respect. In Zuo Fengyi, he skillfully handled corrupt officials like Yang Zhan and Xie You, using subtle pressure to force their resignations, while also reassigning ineffective magistrates to improve governance. Xue Xuan’s fair and compassionate rule, including his handling of a jailer’s suicide, won him praise. He was promoted to Shaofu and later, on Gu Yong’s recommendation, became Imperial Censor and then Prime Minister, enfeoffed as Marquis of Gaoyang. However, his tenure was marred by criticism for being overly meticulous and lacking depth in classical learning. After failing to quell uprisings in Guanghan and mishandling tax collection during a funeral, he was dismissed by the emperor for incompetence. Later reinstated as a special advisor, he was again dismissed for associating with the disgraced Chunyu Chang. His later years were troubled by family discord with his brother Xue Xiu over mourning rites, and his son Xue Kuang’s violent attack on Shen Xian, who had slandered Xue Xuan. Despite legal debates, Xue Kuang was exiled, and Xue Xuan died at home as a commoner.
